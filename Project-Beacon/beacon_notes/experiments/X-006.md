# X-006 Deterministic metadata, re-tested on X-004's labels

Evidence for [[Q-019]]; closing it is the owner's ruling. It supersedes [[X-005]]. X-005's own design was tested once and not met. The owner then changed the design and the labels it was scored on, and a design and a criterion changed after a result belong in a second experiment (`method/experiment-conduct.md`, rule 1). This note is that experiment, and carries the whole account; X-005's note stays as its record.

## Hypothesis

X-005 stated the owner's credit rules to a model and was not met on authors. Deciding each field from deterministic sources instead, with the site's own organisation as the publisher, gets the title, every author and the publication time right on at least 54 of X-004's 60 labelled items and authors on at least 56, and the same page gives the same metadata on every run.

## Setup

### What came before (X-005, 2026-10-01)

X-005 tested, on X-004's 60 labelled items, a design whose model was told the owner's settled credit rules: the page's own byline or labelled author block wins; people first, else the organisations it credits; else agencies that jointly released the item; else the author of a citation the page gives for itself; never a quoted person. Its single run, at `fad09d5`, was **not met**: all three fields right on 54 of 60 (54 needed) and 28 of 30 recent (27 needed), but authors right on 54 of 60 (56 needed). Titles and dates were right on every page X-004 had kept. Of the four authors misses on kept pages, the model gave no credit on two where the name was in its text, credited a person on one page that credits no one, and missed a list of joint authors inside a body.

Its assessments, on 60 development items (X-003's labels) and 30 check items (X-002's): the model was nearly deterministic but sensitive to any change in its input, about one answer in seven moving when the same content was presented differently. It copied names well (49 of 51 where the labelled names were in its text) and judged their role badly: a false credit on 7 of 27 pages that credit no one, most often the site's own name, which was the right answer on 9 other pages. Two structural checks on its answer caught some false credits, losing no real credit, but were shaped on development items. Showing it the page's markup, a second reading by agreement and rewritten prompts each removed real organisation credits along with false ones.

### The design

The owner ruled that the site's own organisation is the publisher, never an author, and that the model leaves every decision a deterministic source can make. This design was first frozen and run inside X-005 (`26f5e30`, its "second freeze"); it is re-homed here unchanged.

- **The rule.** The site's own organisation is never an author; it is recorded as the publisher. It is recognised by equality, case and whitespace aside, with the page's declared site name or publisher, the source's name, or the host label, and removed from every source of author candidates, name by name, before anything is decided. Authors are the people, and organisations other than the site, that the page credits. Agencies a page lists as jointly releasing the item stay authors, and the site's own agency among them is the publisher.
- **Deterministic sources decide, and must not contradict.** Per field, `tiers.json`'s `agreement` section names the voters: for the title, the page's headline and its declared metadata; for authors, declared metadata and the byline element; for the publication time, declared metadata and the listing page, with the date heuristic (htmldate) as the fallback where neither gives a day. The first voter in order wins unless another voter contradicts it. Titles are compared as normalised text with a trailing site name set aside (only across a separator); dates at the precision each source gives, the most precise agreeing value kept; authors as sets of names matched on whole words. A headline found only by searching for the declared title counts as one source with it. The other extractors (trafilatura, Defuddle, and for titles the listing) are recorded as witnesses and never vote: on development and check, trafilatura's date was wrong on all 20 of its disagreements with a voter, Defuddle's on all 6 of its disagreements with the heuristic, and the listing's title on 10 of 29.
- **Every value carries a status:** `agreed` (two voters agree), `single` (one voter), `fallback`, `disputed` (voters contradict; the first in order is kept), `empty`, or `model`.
- **The byline element** is read by a CSS selector per source, held as data in `sources.json`: `author_selector`, with optional `author_separators` for an element holding several names and `author_drop` for descendants to ignore (arXiv's affiliation marks and notes); `null` states that a source's pages carry no byline element, and a missing key is refused. The selectors were found by hand on development pages. This rests on the owner's assumption that configuring a source's page structure is a required upstream step, not yet designed.
- **No model decides a field a deterministic source has a value for.** Two targeted calls exist in the code: on a disputed field, one call for that field alone, which may only choose one of the contradicting candidates or "none", with a quote verified against the page's text, run in advise mode (recorded, never changing a value); and, where a title or date would be empty, a last-resort call that may fill it with a value it quotes from the page, verified and labelled as the model's (never for authors). On all 89 disagreements among sources on development and check, weak ones counted, the first voter in order was right every time, and the escalation call was right on 69, moving under a reorder of the candidates on 14. **This run has both calls off, and calls no model.**
- **The labels** are `labels-reread.json`, built once in X-005 from each saved page's declared metadata, never from the stack's output: each labelled author that is the site's own name is removed, and a label left empty reads "no author". It changed 6 development labels (4 as submitted), 4 check labels and 8 test labels (7 as submitted). On test S086 the site's own agency is one of 26 joint organisations and is removed; the other 25 stay.

**Behaviour inherited from X-005's code and stated nowhere else** (its `T4-NOTES.md`): a source's drop selectors apply inside its matched region, in place, before everything else, and a drop selector naming the region itself is refused; HTML entities are unescaped once, where candidates are gathered; the declared publisher is resolved through JSON-LD `@graph` references.

**This code** is `experiments/X-006/`, package `x006`, copied from X-005's tracked code. Before any change it gave output identical to X-005's on all 90 development and check items. The first design's unused model-decision path was then removed (the credit text, the whole-page model call and its tier, the exploratory orders); X-006's output then differed from X-005's only in six keys that path alone produced (the three fields' `llm_rejected`, `llm_error`, and the context's `credit_text` and `credit_kind`), with no resolved value, provenance, status or deterministic candidate different (`../beacon-scratch/archive/2026-09-30-x005/findings-x005.md`, "X-006: the second design re-homed").

**On development and check,** with no model in any decision: all three right on 59 of 60 and 29 of 30 on the reread labels (titles 60 and 30, authors 60 and 30, dates 59 and 29). Dev S210's date is lost (only X-005's model had it); check S123's date is one its metadata and the heuristic agree on and the label rejects. Statuses over the 270 fields: titles 81 agreed and 9 single; authors 28 agreed, 24 single, 37 empty and 1 disputed; dates 9 agreed, 71 single and 10 fallback. These are in-sample: the selectors were found on these development pages, and which sources may vote was set from these 90 items' disagreements.

### Not held out: the third pass over seen items

X-004's 60 labelled items are the test. They were seen: X-005's first run scored them (54 of 60); their misses drove this design; the labels were reread after that run, under a rule the owner chose then; this design was already run on them once inside X-005 (57 of 60); and building the reread labels and the first run's reference read the test pages' metadata. X-002's 30 check items also chose among designs and stop rules. So this run can show only that the frozen design, re-homed, reproduces on these pages what X-005's second run measured, and that it needs no model. It cannot show accuracy on unseen pages, or on a source nobody has configured.

### The run

`experiments/X-006/test_replay.py --test`, once. It is refused unless this note, `experiments/X-006` (less `runs/` and `out/`) and a `design(X-006)` commit are in HEAD and clean; if any X-006 test manifest exists on disk or in git history; if X-005's first-run manifest is missing; or if the design commit is not later than that first run's. It calls no model, writes its manifest before any work, and counts in the ledger.

It requires `first-run-reference.json`: X-005's first run replayed from its cached model answers through X-005's first freeze (`fad09d5`, in a temporary worktree), refused unless it reproduces that run's recorded per-item verdicts and per-field counts exactly, and holding each deterministic source's candidates per item. The file sits in `experiments/X-006/`, which the freeze requires committed, and the date heuristic's candidate depends on the day of the run, so the reference is rebuilt, the freeze committed and the run made on the same day.

**Invalid** if any item's deterministic candidates for title, authors or date differ from the reference's, with two deliberate exceptions: authors are compared after the publisher rule is applied to the reference's own candidates, and the byline tier, which the first freeze did not have, is not compared.

It reports each field's status and each source's selector rates before the score, scores the decided values on the reread labels, corrected and as submitted, and rescores X-005's first run on them.

**Prediction, written before the run: X-005's second run, figure for figure.**
- On the reread corrected labels: all three right on 57 of 60, recent 30 of 30, titles 58, authors 57, dates 58; the misses S002 and S204 (never kept) and S086 (joint authors inside the body). Criterion met.
- As submitted, reread: all three 56 of 60, recent 30 of 30, titles 58, authors 58, dates 56.
- Statuses on the 58 kept items: titles 55 agreed and 3 single; authors 16 agreed, 14 single, 27 empty and 1 disputed (S056); dates 2 agreed, 49 single and 7 fallback; nothing needing the last resort.
- Selector rates: a match on every page of every source with a selector except 3 of 7 openai pages, where it matches nothing; no selector that decided an item's authors reads a non-name.
- X-005's first run rescored on the reread labels: 51 of 60 corrected and 50 as submitted.

Any difference means the pages were not read the same way or the copy is not faithful, and is reported as such.

**Stop rule.** No change after the run. It is recorded not met if authors fall below 56; if a selector that supplied an item's authors matched an element whose text is not, after splitting, entirely a labelled name or the site's own name; if more than 6 fields are disputed; or if the run is invalid. A selector matching nothing on a page whose declared metadata names a person is reported, not a stop condition. Three choices were made in X-005 after seeing check data, by the owner's rulings of 2026-10-01: the non-name condition first applied to every selector match and was narrowed to items the selector decides, because arXiv's byline element holds affiliation marks on pages whose authors come from metadata; "a selector matches nothing where metadata names a person" was a stop condition and became a reported count, because it fired on an arXiv check page whose authors are right; and arXiv's `author_drop` was added after check data showed those marks.

**Known limits:**
- the author is right only where a source is configured: a byline outside its selector's element, a byline in a bare text node, a source with several templates, or a site redesign gives no author or a wrong one until the upstream step catches it; generality to a source nobody configured is not claimed;
- two selectors are fragile: hiddenlayer's rests on the element's position, calif's on styling classes;
- a list of joint authors inside the body (cisa) is reached by no selector, and the labels for such lists disagree across sets (check S059 says "no one credited", test S086 names 26 organisations);
- a self-citation that names people is not read (every self-citation here names the site, which is the publisher);
- "the site's own name" is recognised by equality only; a byline holding the site's name with other words is taken as an author, and a trailing site name outside the four recognised strings stays in a declared title and makes the title disputed;
- a source configured `null` that later starts printing bylines reports "no author" without notice until the upstream step's checks see it;
- a name in the page's metadata that the page never shows is still reported: declared metadata comes first and is not checked against the page;
- the publisher rule does not distinguish "the page credits its organisation" from "the page credits no one" in the authors field; a `byline` flag records it for the byline element only;
- agreement is not truth: on check S123 the declared date and the heuristic agree on a date the label rejects, and nothing flags it;
- most dates rest on one source (71 of 90 on development and check), so a wrong declared or listing date passes as `single`;
- the date heuristic depends on the day of the run, so the same page could give a different fallback date on another day;
- a date only a model could read is lost (dev S210);
- which sources may vote was set from these 90 items' disagreements; a new source may make a voter wrong, and a dispute is where that would first show;
- the targeted calls are unmeasured as deciders; the escalation call sees printed text only, so a candidate only metadata carries cannot be quoted (in X-005's trial the model answered "none" on 12 of 13 arXiv dates);
- a byline injected only by JavaScript on a page with no rendered copy, or a label printed inside the byline element, is not handled;
- a test page missing on disk counts as absent and scores wrong, rather than making the run invalid: the invalid rule compares only items that ran.

## Criterion

On X-004's 60 labelled items with the owner's corrected labels, each labelled author that is the site's own organisation read as the publisher instead (frozen in labels-reread.json), replayed offline from X-004's saved pages (S002 and S204, never kept, count wrong): the frozen design, deciding each field by its first voter in order that no other voter contradicts, gets the title, every author and the Source publication time all right on at least 54 of 60; all three right on at least 90 per cent of the labelled items published in X-004's recent six months (27 of 30); and authors right on at least 56 of 60.

## Budget and staging

10 minutes of run time, summed across stages and enforced in code by refusing a stage once the ledger's minutes are used (a stage already running is not interrupted); no stage calls a model. Smoke: 3 development items. Test: X-004's 60 items, once, on the day the reference is rebuilt and the freeze committed.

## Result

**Met, as predicted figure for figure.** Run at the freeze (`4d2141f`), once, on 2026-10-01, the day the reference was rebuilt: `experiments/X-006/runs/X-006-test-20261001T193517.json`, after a smoke run on 3 development items (`X-006-smoke-20261001T193505.json`). 58 of the 60 items had pages. No model was called. The run is valid: every item's deterministic candidates equal the reference's. No stop condition fired.

| Clause, on the corrected labels as reread | Needed | Predicted | Measured | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Title, authors and date all right | 54 of 60 | 57 | 57 (Wilson 0.86 to 0.98) | met |
| All three right, recent six months | 27 of 30 | 30 | 30 | met |
| Authors right | 56 of 60 | 57 | 57 | met |

**It reproduces X-005's second run exactly.** The corrected and as-submitted scores, the per-item results, the statuses, the selector rates, the stop rule, the absent items and the rescoring of X-005's first run are each identical to X-005's `test2` manifest:
- titles 58, authors 57, dates 58; as submitted, reread, all three 56, authors 58, dates 56;
- the misses S002 and S204 (never kept) and S086 (joint authors inside the body);
- statuses on the 58 kept items: titles 55 agreed and 3 single; authors 16 agreed, 14 single, 27 empty and 1 disputed (S056, its metadata kept, no call made); dates 2 agreed, 49 single and 7 fallback; nothing needing the last resort;
- every source with a selector matched on every page, except 3 of 7 openai pages, where it matched nothing; no selector that decided an item's authors read a non-name.

**Like for like, on the reread labels.** The model-deciding design of X-005 reads 51 of 60 (authors 51, recent 25 of 30); this design reads 57. Six items are gained and none lost: on S217, S244 and S267 X-005's model credited the site's own name; on S374 and S380 its extractor gave a string holding the site's name; on S261 its model credited a person on a page that credits no one. Five of the six are the publisher rule applied to the output, and one is a model's false credit going. On X-005's own labels it read 54; two items it had wrong, S200 and S268, give "no author" in both designs and are right only because their labels were reread. So most of the gain is the rule the owner chose, applied to labels and output alike; what the mechanism adds is that no model decides.

**Against X-004** (50 of 60 on its labels before rereading), seven of its ten misses read right on the reread labels (S030, S207, S217, S244, S261, S267, S268) and nothing it had right is broken; its remaining three are the two unkept items and S086.

**What the figure is worth.** It is the third pass over 60 items whose misses had been seen, after a redesign informed by them, on labels redefined after the first result, with the selectors and voter roles chosen on 90 items from the same nine sources. It shows that the design, re-homed and frozen, decides these pages' metadata without a model and reproduces X-005's second run in every compared figure: the scores, the per-item verdicts, the statuses, the selector rates and the deterministic candidates (the decided values themselves were not compared). It is a reproduction, not a new measurement of accuracy. Both runs were made on the same day, 2026-10-01, and 7 dates came from the heuristic, whose answer depends on the run day; so the two runs do not show that a run on another day gives the same metadata. It says nothing about unseen pages or a source nobody has configured. Most dates rest on one source (49 of 58), and a wrong single source would pass unflagged.

**Time.** The ledger counts under 1 of the 10 minutes: smoke 10 seconds, the test 38 seconds, no model call.

**For [[Q-019]].** On X-004's items the deterministic design gets all three right on 57 of 60, all 30 recent and authors on 57, with the site's own organisation as the publisher, and reproduces it exactly across two runs on the same day. Against the question's "Answered when", it reports no per-tier rate at all, on the owner's hand labels or otherwise; its scores are on labels reread under the publisher rule, not the hand labels as given; it reports the recent six months for all three fields together, not per tier; and it measures no text extractor, whose rate still rests on [[X-004]]. Whether that answers the question is the owner's ruling.
