# X-003 A pre-registered extraction design on held-out labels

Answers [[Q-019]] and carries its priority, P1. Tests, on labels no design choice has seen, the design that [[X-002]]'s per-tier rates suggested but could not settle ([[ACQ-02]], [[CLS-06]]).

## Hypothesis

Taking each metadata field from a fixed order of tiers, chosen on X-002's labels and frozen before any new label exists, gets the title, every author and the Source publication time right on nearly every new item. The text extractor keeps each class of structure an item's body holds.

## Setup

**Held out means never used to choose the design.** The draw excludes, by canonical address (tracking parameters and a trailing slash set aside), every item that shaped it, and only those:
- X-002's 30 labelled items;
- X-001's 10 hand-checked items;
- the items of X-002's development runs. One early dry run is left out: it kept no sample, and its items shaped no metadata choice.

They are listed, with the reason for each, in `experiments/X-003/exclude.json`. X-002's other items were never labelled, so they may be drawn; their scripted structure scores did inform the choice of text extractor, so held out is claimed for the metadata labels only, not for structure. All nine sources X-002 covered stay in the test; CNA remains uncovered.

**The sample.** A seeded draw (seed 3) takes at most 5 items per source per calendar month from 2026-04-01 to 2026-09-27, as X-002's did. The owner labels 60 of them, allocated as evenly as each source's items allow. 20 of those 60 are also verified for structure: never a document (a URL path ending in a PDF or office file type), and at least 2 per source wherever a source has 2 labelled items that are not documents. The draw ranks each source's eligible items in a seeded order and verifies the first; a verified item that turns out to be a document only when fetched is reported and replaced by its source's next-ranked item.

**The pre-registered design** is frozen in `experiments/X-003/tiers.json` with this note's first design commit, before the draw:

| Field | Tiers, in order |
|---|---|
| Title | headline → declared → model → visible |
| Authors | declared → model → visible |
| Source publication time | declared → listing → heuristic → model → visible |

No generic guess is used. The tiers:
- **declared:** the page's structured metadata;
- **listing:** a publication date the listing itself gives;
- **visible:** defuddle's reading of the byline and date;
- **headline:** the region's first `<h1>`, else the last `<h1>` before the region in document order, else none; rejected when its normalised text equals the page's declared site or publisher name;
- **heuristic:** htmldate's first-published date;
- **model:** a local model that may only quote a span found verbatim in the page.

Every tier runs on every item, so four other orders, named in `tiers.json`, are scored on the same labels as exploratory comparisons. Each tier's candidate for each field is also scored against the labels on its own, and its match rate is reported with a Wilson interval: the per-tier rates [[Q-019]] asks for.

**The text extractor** is the source's content region (its entry's selector, less scripts, forms and the entry's boilerplate), converted to Markdown by markdownify 1.2.3, with formulas written as their LaTeX and tables Markdown cannot hold kept as HTML. Its keep rate is reported for each structure class: headings, lists, tables, code blocks, block quotes, formulas, links, and images with captions.

**Structure is judged per element.** An element counts as kept when its text occurs in the Markdown after both are normalised (NFKC, casefold, letters and digits only): headings by their text, lists per item, tables per non-empty cell, code blocks and block quotes by their text, a link by its text or address, an image by its source or alt text, a caption by its text, and a formula by its LaTeX. Empty elements are not counted.

For each verified item the owner answers three questions, which between them catch a false loss and a false keep:
1. Is the region the article body?
2. Are the losses the script reports really missing?
3. Is anything else of these classes missing or broken?

Completeness and readability notes are recorded and not scored.

**Matching the labels.**
- **Titles** match when their letters and digits agree after NFKC and casefolding, once a trailing segment (after a final `|`, `–`, `-` or `:`) equal to the page's site or publisher name is set aside. Titles are also reported under [[Q-019]]'s own match, with only case, whitespace and a trailing site name set aside.
- **Authors** match when, after NFKC, casefolding and setting aside whitespace and diacritics, each labelled name occurs in exactly one of the record's author strings and each string holds a labelled name.
- **Dates** match on the same calendar day in the record value's own offset.
- A label of none matches an empty record. Otherwise a field counts only when it carries the tier it came from.
- The owner labels the title as the headline the page shows, and the date as the first-published date where a page shows both.

The owner labels in a local form, with typed fields and dates from a date picker, saved as UTF-8; a validator checks the file before scoring.

**The decision rule** is pre-registered with the orders. Each exploratory order is compared with the pre-registered one on the same items by a two-sided exact McNemar test, the family of four Holm-corrected, at α = 0.05, and it needs at least 6 items on which the two disagree. An order that does significantly better is named as a candidate for a later test, never as the answer.

**Dev expectation**, not a criterion: re-scored from X-002's stored candidates, the pre-registered order without the headline tier gets 26 of X-002's 30 labels right on all three fields, against 23 for X-002's order under the same replay.

**Two freezes.** The first design commit freezes this note, `tiers.json`, the decision rule, `exclude.json` and the draw; the owner labels while the extraction and scoring code is built. The second freezes that code before any page is fetched for scoring; it may add to the Setup only, and the criterion, the orders, the decision rule and the exclusions stay as the first froze them. The scorer refuses to run if `tiers.json` differs from the first freeze's.

**Settled by the second freeze**, from X-002's items only:
- **Streamed pages.** Some pages send part of the article in a hidden segment at the end of the body, with a script call that moves it into place. Before the region is taken, each segment is moved to the placeholder the page's own call names. This recovered the quote X-002 lost on one OpenAI post (X-002's S140). Content a page builds only when a reader clicks is not in the page and no check can count it; the third verification question is what catches it.
- **Structure matching, as built.** An element's text is looked for in the Markdown's text, rendered, with and without the numbers of ordered lists, so Markdown's own syntax never counts as text. Addresses and LaTeX are looked for in the Markdown itself. An element whose normalised text is empty is not counted. X-002's link and quote disagreement (S129) came from comparing counts; per element, every link and quote is kept. On X-002's 226 HTML items the check passes 224.
- **The headline** is looked for in the region after the segments are moved.
- **Two rules kept from X-002's cascade.** An author that is the page's own site or publisher name gives way to the next tier's person. Where the listing gives only the month, a visible, heuristic, model or guessed date outside that month is set aside, while a declared date never is. The model's title and date, like its authors, count only when the span it quotes occurs verbatim in the page's header text.
- **Documents.** A document's only title is the listing's, and the pre-registered title order has no listing tier, so a labelled document could not match its title. The draw labelled none.
- **Scoring.** A tier's rate counts the items on which that tier gave a candidate. The all-three count is over all 60 labels, and an item with no record is wrong on every field. The raw-page rate is over every sampled item, and an item a stage never reached counts as not kept.
- **The verification form** asks the three questions with every answer unanswered at first, and the validator refuses an unanswered one.
- **Replay.** Selecting from X-002's stored candidates with X-002's order reproduces X-002's recorded values, on fixtures chosen by the rule each exercises.
- **Dev expectation, as built:** X-002's 30 labelled items re-extracted from their kept pages through this code get all three fields right on 28 under the pre-registered order, 25 without the headline tier and 23 under X-002's order.

**A third freeze, a deviation found at smoke.** The smoke stage's 3 items, none of them labelled or verified, showed that an item whose full text exists only as a PDF never read its listed page's declared metadata. The HTML path read it, the PDF path did not, and X-002 had no such item. On the owner's ruling of 2026-09-29, the PDF path now reads it too, in a third design commit made before any labelled or verified item was fetched. Nothing else changed.

The stack is X-002's otherwise: robots.txt read to RFC 9309, crawl delays honoured, raw pages kept byte for byte, and local models only.

## Criterion

On items drawn by month from the Source Set's April to September 2026 publications, excluding every item that shaped the design: the stack keeps the raw page of at least 95 per cent of the sampled items; the owner confirms the scripted structure check, finding the region to be the article body, the losses it reports real and nothing else of its classes missing or broken, on at least 18 of 20 verified items, and at least 90 per cent of the HTML items keep at least 90 per cent of every structure class present; and the pre-registered tier order gets the title, every author and the Source publication time all right on at least 54 of 60 items the owner labels.

## Budget and staging

120 minutes of run time in all, stopped in code. Smoke: 3 items. Screen: the 20 verified items, so the owner verifies while the rest run. Full: the rest of the sample, in one pass.

## Result

**Not met.** The raw-page and scripted-structure clauses pass; the owner-confirmed structure and metadata clauses do not.

Runs, all on 2026-09-29 (Asia/Singapore): the draw (10:44; 215 picks from 9 sources, 60 to label, 20 to verify), smoke twice (13:52 and, after the third freeze, 14:02; 3 items each), screen (14:04; the 20 verified items, 1.7 minutes), full (14:07; the other 195, 24.6 minutes) and the score (16:04). 29.4 of the 120 budgeted minutes were used.

1. **Raw pages: met.** The stack kept the raw page of all 215 sampled items (100 per cent, against 95), none blocked and none cut off; one arXiv item came only as a PDF and took its metadata from its listed page.
2. **Structure, owner-confirmed: not met.** The owner confirmed the scripted check on 9 of the 20 verified items, against 18. Of the other 11, 7 had something else of the classes missing or broken (page titles outside the region on CISA and The Hacker News items, METR's BibTeX blocks, words run together after bold text), 3 had a region that was not the article body (a fragment on one HiddenLayer post; a "Keep reading" block included on two OpenAI posts) and 1 was a false loss the script reported (arXiv, S026).
3. **Structure, scripted: met.** 211 of the 214 HTML items kept at least 90 per cent of every class present (98.6 per cent, against 90). Per class, the share of items keeping at least 90 per cent: headings, tables, formulas, links, images, captions and block quotes 100 per cent; list items 99.4; code blocks 95.7.
4. **Metadata: not met.** The pre-registered order got the title, every author and the Source publication time all right on 44 of the 60 labels (73 per cent, Wilson 61 to 83), against 54. Titles 57 of 60 (56 under [[Q-019]]'s own match), publication times 59 of 60, authors 47 of 60. Twelve of the 16 misses are authors on two sources: METR (0 of 6 items all right) and OpenAI (1 of 7). The other seven sources got 43 of their 47 items all right.

The decision rule: no exploratory order did significantly better than the pre-registered one. X-002's order did significantly worse (30 of 60; 16 items against it, 2 for it; Holm-adjusted p = 0.005), as did the declared-first title order in direction but not significantly (36 of 60; p = 0.12). Putting the model before the heuristic for dates (45 of 60) and the visible byline before the model for authors (42 of 60) made no significant difference.

How often each tier's candidate matched the label, over the items where it gave one:

| Field | declared | listing | visible | headline | heuristic | model | guessed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Title | 49 of 60 | 13 of 20 | 56 of 60 | 52 of 55 | — | 48 of 55 | 47 of 60 |
| Authors | 14 of 27 | — | 22 of 24 | — | — | 26 of 32 | 12 of 33 |
| Publication time | 20 of 20 | 33 of 33 | 32 of 36 | — | 39 of 47 | 42 of 43 | 41 of 60 |

The dev expectation was 28 of 30 on X-002's items; the held-out rate, 44 of 60, is well below it, so the design fitted the items it was chosen on better than it generalises.

The labels were reviewed, after both freezes and on the owner's request, by checks independent of the stack and by listing the fields no tier matched; the owner corrected three dates to the first-published date, answered one unanswered question and made one verification consistent with a like item. On the owner's rulings, four verifications' third answer changed from No to Yes after the files were found to contradict every problem their notes named (the notes matched a rendered Markdown preview, not the file; S006's tables are kept as HTML, which holds their merged cells). The corrected notes keep the owner's text, and the score's manifest carries the final files. The verification form was laid out from the screen stage's records by a helper kept outside the frozen tree; it adds nothing the records do not hold.

What the design cannot show: content a page builds only on a click (OpenAI's tabbed quotes); whether HTML tables inside Markdown suit BEACON's later stages; author credits a page renders late or places outside its header, which the owner labelled from the page and no tier found; and, with 6 or 7 labels per source, any per-source rate beyond the direction of the METR and OpenAI misses.
