# X-002 Metadata and extraction over six months of the Source Set

Answers [[Q-019]] and carries its priority, P1. Follows [[X-001]], whose third part was not met, over the Source Set that [[Q-005]] settled.

## Hypothesis

A cascade that takes each metadata field from the first tier that has it, and labels that tier, gets the title, every author and the Source publication time right on nearly every item ([[ACQ-02]]), where X-001's single generic guess did not. The tiers, in order: declared on the page (JSON-LD, OpenGraph, citation meta); the listing; visible on the page (defuddle's reading of the byline and date); a date heuristic (htmldate, first-published date); a local language model reading the page's header (qwen3:8b); a generic extractor's guess (trafilatura). Meanwhile X-001's region extractor keeps the structure of items over six months of template changes ([[CLS-06]]).

## Setup

The Source Set's ten sources, collected with X-001's stack copied to `experiments/X-002/`, changed where X-001 showed it wrong:

- **Metadata by provenance.** Every tier runs on every item and its candidate is kept, so each tier's rate is measured on the same items; the chosen value is the first acceptable candidate in tier order, labelled `declared`, `listing`, `visible`, `heuristic`, `llm` or `guessed`. Every author is kept as a list, without affiliations; an author that is the page's own site or publisher name gives way to a later tier's person. The language model may only extract: it returns, with the names, the exact span of the page that credits them, and a name is accepted only if that span occurs verbatim in the page's header text and contains the name. No list of byline words, job titles or date formats is used anywhere. A listing date counts as a listing candidate only when it is a publication date the listing itself gives (a feed's, a listing page's, a URL's, CISA's `<time>`, CSA's payload), not a sitemap's modification date or a date read from the item page; where the listing gives only the month (The Hacker News, arXiv), a visible, heuristic, model or guessed date outside that month is set aside, while a declared date never is. Dates keep their own offset.
- **arXiv.** Its metadata comes from the abstract page, its text from the HTML full text.
- **The scripted comparison is corrected.** Empty list items and headings are not counted as elements, and link titles are removed before captions are matched. Both flaws were found in X-001.
- **Scanned PDF pages** are rendered locally and read by a local vision model (qwen2.5vl:7b), each page labelled `text` or `ocr`. PDFs stay out of the structure sample.
- **The models.** Chosen on evidence of fitness for the job, the owner's ruling of 2026-09-28, and run locally on the machine's 8 GB GPU; no hosted service is used. OCR: qwen2.5vl:7b, whose model family leads Gemini 1.5 Pro on OCRBench v2 by 9.6 points in English (arXiv:2502.13923). Metadata: qwen3:8b, the strongest at instruction following and JSON-schema output among models that fit the GPU, run with thinking off at temperature 0.

**The sample.** Each source's items from 2026-04-01 to 2026-09-27 are listed through an archive route (a sitemap with dates, a full feed, a paged listing, or arXiv's monthly lists), found by probing before the design is frozen. A seeded draw (seed 2) takes at most 5 items per source per calendar month, spread across each month's thirds; where only the month is known (The Hacker News, arXiv), by a seeded shuffle. A month is *covered* when the source's route lists a dated item in it, or lists an earlier item and none in it (the source published nothing that month); otherwise it is uncovered, and the reason is recorded. An item's month comes from its listed date: the sitemap's publication date or `lastmod`, the feed's date, the paged listing's date, the date in its URL, arXiv's list month, or, for HiddenLayer, the item page's JSON-LD; an item with none of these is not drawn, and a source whose route dates nothing covers no month. The draw is made from the listings before any item is fetched, except HiddenLayer's, whose item pages are fetched for their dates. An item robots.txt refuses is not fetched and counts as not kept.

**The truth for metadata is the owner's hand labels,** not what the page declares, which is the blind spot X-001 showed: 30 items, 3 from each source that has 3 (from different months where it allows), topped up from the sources with the most items, drawn by the same seed from the sampled items and labelled from the live page; 10 of them, one per labelled source and topped up the same way, also get a structure hand check. A field *matches* when titles are alike after case, whitespace and anything after a final `|` are set aside; authors match when, after case, whitespace and diacritics are set aside, each labelled name occurs in exactly one of the record's author strings and each record string holds a labelled name, and a label of none matches an empty record; and dates fall on the same calendar day, in the record value's own offset, the first-published date where a page shows both. Each of the three fields must also carry a provenance label for the item to count, except a field rightly empty. The structure hand check agrees with the script when the owner's mark and the script's verdict on the item are the same. Thirty labels screen the metadata rate rather than settle it (27 of 30 has a 95 per cent interval of about 74 to 97 per cent); the per-tier rates are the evidence the Extractor is designed on, and the owner ruled the 30 on 2026-09-28. Claude may open the label pages in Chrome for the owner, but writes no label.

**What probing found before the freeze.** Nine of the ten sources have an archive route: sitemaps (METR, The Hacker News, The Straits Times), a full feed (OpenAI), paged listings (CISA, HiddenLayer, where each item's date comes from its own page), the listing already used (Calif), arXiv's monthly lists, and CSA's listing page, whose static HTML embeds every item with its date (found by inspecting the page in Chrome). The Hacker News and arXiv give only the month. CNA has none: its older items come only from an interface its robots.txt disallows, so it counts as uncovered. Probing also showed that the robots.txt parser X-001 used ignores the standard's wildcards (RFC 9309); X-002 uses one that honours them. Rechecked with it, no URL X-001's last full run fetched was disallowed. The scanned-page setting (render scale 2, a 16,384-token context) was measured on a page of CSA's bulletin: 99.3 per cent of its words agreed with the page's text layer. That bulletin itself needs no OCR, because its text layer is whole; a generic converter misread its glyph mapping.

**The one pass.** There are no repeat runs, since X-001 covered repeat visits. The stack honours robots.txt and crawl delays, keeps raw pages byte for byte, and records tool and model versions in each manifest. The trial list and code are frozen before the screen run. The result reports, for each field and tier, how often that tier's candidate matched the label: the evidence a routed Extractor would be designed on.

## Criterion

On a sample drawn by month from what the Source Set's ten sources published from 2026-04-01 to 2026-09-27, at most 5 items per source per month: the stack's archive routes cover every month for at least 8 of the 10 sources, and keeps the raw page of at least 95 per cent of the sampled items; on the HTML items, the extracted text keeps each class of element in the item's body (headings, lists, tables, code blocks, block quotes, formulas, links, and images with their captions) at 90 per cent on at least 90 per cent of items, by a scripted comparison that counts only if it agrees with the owner's hand check on at least 9 of 10 items; and on 30 items the owner labels by hand, the record's title, every author and Source publication time all match the labels on at least 27, each field labelled with the tier it came from.

## Budget and staging

120 minutes of run time in all, stopped in code. Smoke: 3 items. Screen: the items drawn from September 2026, in one pass. Full: the items drawn from April to August 2026, in one pass, started as soon as the screen ends; the owner approved the stages, the full one expected at 15 to 20 minutes, with the plan on 2026-09-28. The screen's items count towards the result.

## Result

**Not met.** The first part passes; the second and third do not.

Runs, all on 2026-09-28 (Asia/Singapore): the draw (18:50; 226 picks, 30 to label, 10 structure checks), smoke (3 items), screen (September, 33 items, 2.8 minutes), and full (April to August, 193 items, 20.8 minutes), in one pass with no waiting between stages; about 24 of the 120 budgeted minutes were used. Manifests: `experiments/X-002/runs/`.

1. **Coverage and keeping: met.** Archive routes covered every month from April to September 2026 for 9 of the 10 sources (CNA's older items come only from an interface its robots.txt disallows), and the raw page of all 226 sampled items was kept (100 per cent, against 95).
2. **Structure: not met.** The corrected scripted comparison passed 220 of the 226 HTML items (97.3 per cent, against 90), but it agreed with the owner's hand check on 7 of 10, against 9 needed, so it does not count. The three disagreements are judgements, not errors of either side: the owner failed an arXiv paper (S001) whose LaTeX conversion reads awkwardly and an OpenAI post (S140) whose tabbed quotes were lost, both of which the script passed on element counts; and the owner passed a METR report (S129) the script failed on links and block quotes (593 of 777 links, 3 of 5 quotes).
3. **Metadata: not met.** Title, every author and Source publication time all matched the owner's labels, each with its tier labelled, on 25 of 30 items, against 27.

How often each tier's candidate matched the label, over the 30 labelled items (every tier ran on every item; matching as defined in the Setup):

| Field | declared | listing | visible | heuristic | llm | guessed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Title | 27 of 30 | 5 of 9 | 26 of 30 | — | 27 of 30 | 26 of 30 |
| Authors | 13 of 15 | — | 11 of 12 | — | 14 of 16 | 11 of 18 |
| Publication time | 12 of 12 | 15 of 15 | 14 of 17 | 18 of 21 | 24 of 28 | 18 of 30 |

The llm tier's verification rejected 3 author lists, of which 1 would have matched.

The six failed fields, and what the tiers held:

- In four, a lower tier held the right value and the tier order chose a wrong one: an author where the visible byline was wrong and the model's verified name right (Calif, S053); two titles where the page's declared title differs from its visible headline, which the owner labelled and the model read (HiddenLayer, S120 and S123); a date where the visible tier was a day out and the heuristic, the model and the guess were right (The Hacker News, S212).
- In one, no tier held the labelled date (HiddenLayer, S123).
- In one, the check itself fell short: the page's title has a non-breaking hyphen and the label an ordinary one, which the title match does not treat as alike (OpenAI, S140).

What this shows for the Extractor ([[Q-019]]): declared and listing dates were right every time they existed; the visible tier (defuddle) is the weakest source of dates and author names that looks reliable, and would rank below the heuristic and the model; for titles, a page's declared title can differ from the headline a reader sees; the model, allowed only to quote the page, was right on 27 titles, 14 author lists and 24 dates. These are rates over 30 items, a screen rather than a settled rate.

The labels: the owner's sheet was saved by Excel as Windows-1252, with dates rewritten as day/month/year and eleven dates filled as a daily series. On the owner's rulings of 2026-09-28 the file was converted to UTF-8, arXiv author separators changed from commas to semicolons, the eleven dates re-checked by the owner against the pages, and every date converted to `YYYY-MM-DD`. The owner's files are kept beside it. The first score, on the uncorrected sheet (11 of 30), and the re-score are both in `runs/`.

What the design cannot show: whether the tier rates hold on sources beyond these nine; the per-source differences, since three labels per source are too few; and a site's archive route over years rather than months.
