# X-004 A second held-out extraction design, over twelve months

Answers [[Q-019]] and carries its priority, P1. It follows [[X-003]], which was not met, and changes only what X-003's evidence names ([[ACQ-02]], [[CLS-06]]).

## Hypothesis

Fixing the general causes behind X-003's misses gets title, every author and the Source publication time right on nearly every new item, from October 2025 to the draw and within its most recent six months. The causes: credits outside the header or rendered late; organisations standing in for people; a region that starts below the headline or runs into lists of other items; words joined at inline boundaries. The fixed extractor also keeps each class of structure that an item's body holds, as the owner judges it by a written rubric.

## Setup

**Held out means never used to choose the design.** The draw excludes, by the same canonical key as X-003's, every item X-001, X-002 or X-003 used: X-001's hand-checked items, X-002's labels and development runs, and all 215 of X-003's picks, including those never labelled. That is 263 items in `experiments/X-004/exclude.json`. The design was developed on X-003's 60 labels and 20 verifications. X-002's 30 labels, of which the owner is not certain, were replayed as a check; they shaped two rulings and one gate, and chose nothing else: the organisation rule, where the owner weighed the check's figures (26 against 24 of 30) beside X-003's; the joint-agency ruling, prompted by one of its items; and the citation gate, which ran over its items as well as X-003's. Held out is claimed for the metadata labels. X-002's unlabelled items may be drawn, and their structure scores informed X-003's choice of text extractor, so structure is not claimed held out.

**The window and its coverage.** A seeded draw (seed 4) takes at most 5 items per source per calendar month from 2025-10-01 to the day before the draw. Archive reach was probed before the freeze. Every source's route reaches the window's start except where a source's own history is shorter: Calif's listing begins in 2026-03. HiddenLayer's listing ignores its page parameter, so it is read from its sitemap and dated from each item page. METR and HiddenLayer have no unseen items in the six months before the draw, so the recent clause runs over the other sources, and the Result names them. Its structure part counts every sampled recent HTML item, not only the labelled ones: more items give a firmer figure. CNA remains uncovered. The owner labels 60 items: 30 published in the six months before the draw and 30 in the six before those, each half allocated across sources as evenly as their items allow. The probe found at least 6,222 unseen recent items on six sources, 481 of them outside arXiv (`findings-x004.md`), so the recent half is expected to hold 30. Were it short, the other half would fill it and the draw would record the split. The owner verifies 20 of the 60 for structure: never a document, and at least 2 per source wherever a source has 2 such items.

**The pre-registered design** is frozen in `experiments/X-004/tiers.json`:

| Field | Tiers, in order |
|---|---|
| Title | headline → declared → model → visible |
| Authors | declared → model → visible |
| Source publication time | declared → listing → model → heuristic → visible |

X-003's order, with the model's dates placed before the heuristic's: on the development items the model's dates matched 50 of 54 and the heuristic's 39 of 47. Each tier's candidate is also scored against the labels on its own, with a Wilson interval, for the whole window and for the recent and older six months apart: the per-tier rates [[Q-019]] asks for. Four other orders are scored as exploratory comparisons: X-003's own; each field's tiers ranked by their development rates; visible before model for authors; and declared before headline for titles.

**What changed from X-003, each a general mechanism:**
- **Rendered pages.** Every HTML item keeps a rendered copy (real Chrome, after network idle or 10 seconds) beside its raw page, together with each `<h1>`'s visible text. A render failure keeps the static record and says so.
- **Credit text.** The author, title and date tiers of the model are checked against the page's credit text. It is built from the rendered page's body, never `<head>`: the header around the headline, the text after the region, and the short lines in the region's header and footer zones, above its first body paragraph and below its last. The header and the tail each have their own budget, and short lines are taken from both ends of the region inward. Page chrome is dropped first: script and style text, navigation, footers, the site's own header, dialogs, and link text that is a bare address. Headings, quote markup, long body blocks and link-dense lists of other items are never credit lines. A model span counts only when it occurs verbatim, whitespace aside, in the credit text.
- **Organisations.** A credit is recognised as the organisation when it matches the page's declared site or publisher name, the source's name, or the item's host label. An organisation gives way to any tier's person. An organisation-only credit counts only when the model credited it with a byline span verified in the credit text; declared metadata alone never keeps an organisation. This applies the owner's ruling that a page crediting its organisation and no person is credited to the organisation. Organisations include agencies the page says jointly released the item, and, when there is no byline, the author of a citation the page gives for itself (a "Cite" or BibTeX block).
- **The headline.** The headline is the region's one `<h1>`, else the last `<h1>` before the region. A region holding several `<h1>`s is using them as section headings. Its text is the rendered visible text when that is contained in the heading, which leaves out hidden footnotes. It is still rejected when it equals the page's site or publisher name.
- **The region.** The body runs from the headline to the region's end, bringing in the title and standfirst. Leading and trailing blocks whose link density is at least 0.9 (trafilatura's `LINK_FARM_RATIO`) are dropped: a table of contents, or a list of other items. `form` and `template` are unwrapped rather than dropped, so a `<pre>` inside one survives. A space is kept where an inline element's end meets a block or a line break (markdownify issue #249).
- **The check.** Structure is judged per element as in X-003. An item also fails when the headline is missing from the Markdown, or when a Markdown word joins two words that the page lays out apart (at a block boundary or a line break), parts that are only digits aside.

**The verification rubric** (`experiments/X-004/rubric.md`) is shown in the form. The body is the article's own title and content, header lines included, and never lists of other items or page furniture such as share widgets and citation popups. A table kept as HTML with every cell intact is kept. `extracted.md` is judged as text. Each card shows the rendered Markdown beside its source, with the three questions of X-003. The form saves as the owner goes.

**Matching the labels** is X-003's, unchanged. If the owner asks for a review of the labels after labelling, it uses only checks that do not consult the stack's output (format, consistency across like items, dates outside the drawn month), never a list of fields where the stack disagrees, which would pull corrections toward the stack. The first validated labels and verifications are kept as submitted, and the score reports the criterion on them as well as on any corrected ones. The owner labels as in X-003, in a local form whose help says what counts as an author: a person the page credits; else an organisation it credits, including agencies it says jointly released the item; else, with no byline, the author of the page's citation for itself.

**The decision rule** is X-003's: a two-sided exact McNemar test for each exploratory order against the pre-registered one, the family Holm-corrected, α = 0.05, with at least 6 discordant items. An order that does significantly better is named as a candidate for a later test, never as the answer.

**Dev expectation**, not a criterion: on X-003's 60 items, re-fetched and rendered, the pre-registered order gets all three fields right on 56 of 60, against 44 for X-003's frozen records. Of the four misses, one item was labelled with no author before the citation ruling, and its page gives a citation naming the organisation; in one the model missed three named people; and in two the model did not credit an organisation the page shows under "Author". The line rule this design replaced got those two right, but by construction it credited a short copyright line standing on its own and missed organisation credits outside the article (`findings-x004.md`); on these items it scored 57 of 60 and 24 of 30 on the check (measured 2026-09-30, `findings-x004.md`). The owner chose the model-credited rule. The rule's gate was whether the model would quote a citation or copyright line as a byline: of the model's 77 bylines on the 90 items, 4 quoted METR's visible citation block and none a copyright line, and the owner ruled that a page's citation for itself counts. On X-002's 30, the check, it gets 26 of 30. Of its four misses, one label is a day-month swap, one predates the joint-agency ruling, one is the "Discovered by" limit below, and in one the model's organisation credit failed verification. These are the items the fixes were developed on. What X-003 showed, 28 of 30 on its own items against 44 of 60 held out, means a gap of that size is possible. For structure, the scripted check passes all 20 of X-003's verified items with this code (`findings-x004.md`, 2026-09-30), against the owner's 9 of 20 under X-003's; how many the owner would now confirm is not measured.

**Known limits, not fixed:**
- a quoted person whose attribution line carries no quote markup can pass as an author;
- a site handle that the page's visible byline gives beside the person is kept;
- the model can miss a credit it was shown, and an organisation-only credit rests on its judgement: a short copyright line the model quotes as a byline would credit the organisation;
- a credit naming someone for another role ("Discovered by") can pass as an author;
- content a page builds only on a click is not in the page;
- every source has a content selector found by hand, in `sources.json`, so X-004 tests new items from known sources, not a source nobody has configured. A later experiment on unconfigured sources, with no hand-found selector, would test that.

**One freeze.** The note, `tiers.json`, the rubric, `exclude.json` and all code are committed together before the draw. The draw, the runs and the scorer refuse unless the commit holds them unchanged. A fix after smoke is recorded as a deviation in a later design commit, as in X-003.

**A deviation during the full stage, the budget only.** The first full run was stopped by the session tool's time limit after 36 items, with no manifest written, so the budget ledger did not count its roughly 30 minutes. The second slowed to about 40 seconds an item: with both local models resident on one 8 GB GPU, the idle OCR model held its memory and the metadata model ran on the CPU (one call took 123 seconds against about 1.4). It was stopped after 85 minutes with no manifest. The machine now keeps one model loaded at a time (`OLLAMA_MAX_LOADED_MODELS=1`, a setting outside the frozen code). On the owner's ruling of 2026-09-30, the budget rises from 150 to 240 minutes in a design commit made before the full stage is run again; the criterion, the draw, the orders and the code are unchanged. The Result reports the true time of every run, the stopped ones included.

The stack is X-003's otherwise: robots.txt read to RFC 9309, crawl delays honoured, raw pages kept byte for byte, and local models only.

## Criterion

On items drawn by month from the Source Set's publications from 2025-10-01 to the day before the draw, excluding every item X-001, X-002 and X-003 used: the stack keeps the raw page of at least 95 per cent of the sampled items; the owner confirms the scripted structure check, finding the region to be the article body as the rubric defines it, the losses it reports real and nothing else of its classes missing or broken, on at least 18 of 20 verified items; at least 90 per cent of the HTML items keep at least 90 per cent of every structure class present; the pre-registered tier order gets the title, every author and the Source publication time all right on at least 54 of 60 items the owner labels; and, on the labelled items published in the six months before the draw, the order gets all three right on at least 90 per cent (27 of 30 if that half is full), and at least 90 per cent of the sampled HTML items published in those six months keep at least 90 per cent of every class present.

## Budget and staging

240 minutes of run time in all, stopped in code (150 as first frozen, raised by the deviation above); rendering every page roughly doubles X-003's fetch time. Smoke: 3 items. Screen: the 20 verified items, so the owner verifies while the rest run. Full: the rest of the sample, in one pass.

## Result

**Not met.** The raw-page and all three structure clauses pass; the two metadata clauses do not.

Runs, all on 2026-09-30 (Asia/Singapore), on `design(X-004)` and its deviation:
- the draw (13:34): 389 picks, 60 to label (30 recent, 30 older), 20 to verify;
- smoke (13:43, 2.2 minutes, 2 items before its cap);
- screen (13:51, 3.8 minutes, the 20 verify items);
- two stopped full runs with no manifest: the session tool stopped the first at about 30 minutes, and the second was stopped after 85 minutes, slowed by GPU contention;
- the completed full run (16:01, 174.1 minutes, 369 items);
- the score.

True run time: about 303 minutes, against a budget of 240 (150 as first frozen). The code's ledger counts about 188 (the draw's 7.9, smoke, screen and full), because it does not see the stopped runs; on the owner's ruling the last run was let finish, and the overrun is recorded here.

1. **Raw pages: met.** 387 of 389 kept (99.5 per cent, against 95). The two misses are both verify items:
   - arXiv S002 hit a read timeout and was not retried;
   - METR S204 is on a page template its content selector does not match.
2. **Structure, owner-confirmed: met.** The owner confirmed 18 of 20, all 18 that could be verified; the two not kept count as not confirmed. The owner confirmed 9 of 20 in X-003.
3. **Structure, scripted: met.** 377 of 381 HTML items kept at least 90 per cent of every class present (99.0 per cent, against 90). Per class, the share of items keeping 90 per cent was:
   - 100 per cent for headings, tables, formulas, images, captions and block quotes;
   - 99.7 for list items and links;
   - 90.0 for code blocks.
4. **Structure, recent six months: met.** 98.9 per cent of the sampled recent HTML items.
5. **Metadata: not met.** The pre-registered order got title, every author and the Source publication time all right on 50 of 60 corrected labels (Wilson 72 to 91 per cent), against 54, and on 49 of the owner's labels as first submitted. By field: titles 56, authors 52, dates 58. The development expectation was 56 of 60, so the held-out figure fell six below it.
6. **Metadata, recent six months: not met.** 26 of the 30 recent labelled items, against 27. The same figure on the labels as first submitted. All four recent misses are OpenAI items.

The ten misses on the corrected labels:
- **two items not kept** (S002, S204), wrong on every field;
- **five authors:**
  - three where the page credits the organisation in an "Author" block but the model did not credit it (OpenAI);
  - one where the model named a person that a subtitle mentions, not the Author block's organisation;
  - one where the model took a person quoted in a customer story;
- **one joint advisory** whose 26 authoring organisations no tier produced;
- **two titles:** one where the page's headline repeats a formula's letter ("KK-Means"), and one where the declared title drops the visible headline's "Research note:" prefix.

The decision rule: no exploratory order did significantly better. X-003's order tied (50 of 60). Declared before headline for titles did worse (41 of 60, Holm-adjusted p = 0.047).

Each tier's rate over the items where it gave a candidate (whole window):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Title | 46 of 58 | 15 of 22 | 54 of 58 | 54 of 55 | — | 35 of 55 | 45 of 58 |
| Authors | 17 of 24 | — | 22 of 25 | — | — | 28 of 32 | 13 of 27 |
| Publication time | 17 of 17 | 33 of 33 | 27 of 29 | — | 37 of 44 | 44 of 47 | 42 of 58 |

The recent and older six months are reported apart in the score manifest; the headline, listing and model date tiers hold in both.

**The labels.**
- **Frozen as submitted.** The owner's labels and verifications were frozen as submitted at their first clean validation. Two verify items not kept, which the form could not show, were added before that as not confirmed.
- **Reviewed on all 60.** On the owner's request, every label was then checked against its page alone, never against the stack's output. Three corrections were logged:
  - S086's authors, set to its 26 authoring organisations under the joint-agency ruling;
  - S064's date, set to the page's March 3;
  - S086's date, set to its initial-version date under the first-published rule.

  A fourth correction, found by the decision review, is S002's date. It was set to arXiv's version-1 date (3 October 2025), which the page-only check had skipped because S002 was not kept. It changes no figure. None of the ten misses above is a labelling error. The score manifest carries both label sets, and the changed ids.

**What this shows for [[Q-019]].** Structure met its bar on this sample, from configured sources: the owner confirmed all 18 verifiable items, exactly the threshold, and structure is not claimed held out. Titles (56 of 60) and dates (58 of 60) are close. Of the ten metadata misses:
- two are items not kept, one by a timeout and one by a page template the selector misses;
- four are the model failing to apply rules already settled: an organisation credited in an "Author" block (three), and a subtitle's person taken over the Author block (one);
- one is the known limit of a quoted person;
- one is a joint advisory's 26 organisations, which no tier produced;
- two are titles.
The gap is mostly the model's judgement in applying the credit rules, not rules left unsettled.

What the design cannot show:
- sources nobody has configured;
- content built only on a click;
- a second labeller;
- the recent six months for METR and HiddenLayer, which had no unseen recent items.
