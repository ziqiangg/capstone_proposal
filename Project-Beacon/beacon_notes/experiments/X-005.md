# X-005 Applying the credit rules, re-tested on X-004's labels

Evidence for [[Q-019]]; closing it is the owner's separate ruling. It follows [[X-004]], which was not met on metadata (50 of 60), four of its ten misses the model not applying credit rules the owner had already settled, and two of them title defects.

## Hypothesis

X-004's prompt asked the model for "the author names" and never stated the rules. Stating them, checking the model's credit against the page's own structure, and fixing the two title defects (a formula's letter collected twice, a headline lost on a page with no `<h1>`) gets title, every author and the date right on at least 54 of X-004's 60 labelled items.

After the first test run (below, under Result), the owner kept X-005 open and redesigned how the metadata is decided: the model's judgement of whether the site's own name is a credit was its largest remaining error and was not reproducible, and the metadata is the first step of a pipeline with many downstream processes. The design frozen second takes the model out of every decision a deterministic source can make; a model fills a title or a date only when none has a value, which never happened on development or check. The site's own organisation is the publisher, never an author; the author credit is read from the page's structure, through a selector per source held as site knowledge in `sources.json`; and title, authors and date are each decided by deterministic sources that must not contradict one another. The hypothesis of the second freeze: with that, X-004's items meet the criterion (the prediction is authors 57 of 60 against a bar of 56, the ceiling less the joint list no selector reaches), and the same page always gives the same metadata.

## Setup

**The design as frozen second (2026-10-01; `../beacon-scratch/archive/2026-09-30-x005/design-ab-x005.md` and `plan-agreement-x005.md` with its addendum).** It supersedes the first design's way of choosing each field, described further down and kept as the record of how it was reached. The body extraction and the title's headline rules are the first design's, unchanged.
- **Deterministic sources decide, and must not contradict.** Per field, `tiers.json` names the sources that may vote: for the title, the page's headline and its declared metadata; for authors, declared metadata and the byline element; for the date, declared metadata and the listing page, with the date heuristic (htmldate) as the fallback where neither gives a day. The first voter in order wins if no other voter contradicts it. Titles are compared as normalised text with a trailing site name set aside; dates at the precision each source gives; authors as sets of names. The other sources (trafilatura, Defuddle, and for titles the listing page) are recorded as witnesses and never vote. On development and check, trafilatura's date was wrong on all 20 of its disagreements with a voter and Defuddle's on all 6 of its disagreements with the heuristic; the listing's title was wrong on 10 of 29; and Defuddle's byline, one unsplit string, "disagrees" with the selector on 13 items where both are right. A headline found only by searching for the declared title counts as one source with it, not two.
- **Every value carries a status:** `agreed` (two voters agree), `single` (one voter has a value), `fallback`, `disputed` (voters contradict; the first in order is kept), `empty`, or `model` (a title or date the last resort filled). Downstream can tell a corroborated value from an uncorroborated one.
- **No model decides a field any deterministic source has a value for.** The whole-page model call of the first design is not made. Two targeted calls exist and are recorded when used:
  - on a disputed field, one call for that field alone, shown the page's text and the contradicting candidates, may only choose one of them or "none", with a quote verified against the text. It runs in advise mode: recorded, never changing a value. On all 89 disagreements among sources on development and check (weak ones counted), the first voter in order was right every time and this call was right on 69, moving under a reorder of the candidates on 14, so letting it decide is a separate experiment on new sources;
  - where a title or a date would be empty, a last-resort call for that field may fill it with a value it quotes from the page's text, verified and labelled as the model's. Authors never get one: an empty author is usually the true answer. It never fires on development or check.
- **The rule.** The site's own organisation (the page's declared site name or publisher, the source's name, or the host label) is never an author; it is recorded as the publisher. Authors are the people, and organisations other than the site, the page credits. Agencies a page lists as jointly releasing the item stay authors (the earlier ruling), and the site's own agency among them is the publisher.
- **The author's sources, in order:** declared metadata, then the page's byline element. No model and no third-party extractor decides an author.
- **The byline element** is found by a CSS selector per source, `author_selector` in `sources.json`, with optional `author_separators` for an element holding several names and optional `author_drop` for descendants to ignore (arXiv's affiliation marks and notes); `null` states that a source's pages carry no byline. A missing key is refused. The selectors were found by hand on development pages. On the check's pages, a hold-out for them, they matched the labelled names wherever the selector decided the authors; arXiv's selector, which metadata always outranks, was right on only 3 of 6 check pages before `author_drop` was added, still disputes metadata on 2, and finds no byline element on 1. The owner's assumption: configuring a source's page structure is a required upstream step, not yet designed, so the code holds no site knowledge and an unconfigured source is outside the claim.
- **The rule in one place.** The site's own name is removed from every source of author candidates, name by name, before selection. A `byline` flag records whether the page's byline element named someone, named only the site, or named no one; it is recorded, never scored.
- **The labels are reread once, mechanically, and frozen** in `labels-reread.json` (built by `build_labels_reread.py` from each saved page's declared metadata, never from the stack's output): each labelled author that is the site's own name is removed, and a label left empty reads "no author". It changed 6 development labels (4 as submitted), 4 check labels, and 8 test labels (7 as submitted). On test S086 the site's own agency is one of the 26 joint organisations and is removed; the other 25 stay.
- **On development and check, with no model in any decision:** all three 59 of 60 and 29 of 30 on the reread labels; titles 60 and 30, authors 60 and 30, dates 59 and 29. The one date lost against the first design is dev S210, which only the model had and no deterministic source gives; the check's miss (S123) is a date its metadata and the heuristic agree on and the label rejects. Statuses over the 270 fields: titles 81 agreed and 9 single; authors 28 agreed, 24 single, 37 empty and 1 disputed (an arXiv check page, S002, the declared winner right); dates 9 agreed, 71 single and 10 fallback. In the measured replay two fields were disputed and two advise-mode calls were made (one verified choice, one "none"), changing nothing; a review fix that compares names by whole words instead of substrings then resolved one of the two. These figures are in-sample: the selectors were found on these development pages, and which sources may vote was set from these 90 items' disagreements.

**What changed from X-004, each a general mechanism:**
- **The credit prompt** states the owner's rules (rulings of 2026-09-29 and 30, ruling 6 of 2026-09-30) in neutral words, with no source, label text or example from the data. The rules: the page's own author credit, its byline or a block it labels as its author credit, wins. Give the people it names; if it names only organisations, those organisations. With no such credit, the organisations the page says jointly released the item. With neither, the author of a citation the page gives for itself. Never a person the page quotes or names in another role. The reply adds the kind of credit (person, organisation, citation, none); its only use is to turn a citation's "Last, First" into "First Last". Every reply field is required by the schema and named in the prompt: on the first development run, a schema with optional fields let the model return the kind alone. The reply is written in the schema's order: the names, at once the span that must hold them, the kind, then title and date. The context length and the reply length are pinned (4,096 and 1,024 tokens), so a server default cannot change what the model reads. Temperature stays 0, which makes a run nearly reproducible; the model's card advises against it.
- **Declared metadata.** One reader for JSON-LD and meta tags; lenient JSON-LD parsing; a node typed with an object no longer discards the tier; a URL given as an author is a profile link, not a name, in JSON-LD as in meta tags.
- **The organisation rule, stated once.** An organisation from declared metadata or the visible extractor gives way to a later tier's person, as before. An organisation the model credits with a verified span is kept, in the model tier's place. X-004's separate fallback layer is deleted.
- **The credit text** is X-004's, except that its header is the text before the content region inside `<body>`, the last third of the cap, rather than a window around the first `<h1>` found by a string search (removing a re-parse and five unjustified constants). Each short credit line is given once, as the outermost element that qualifies, in document order, with the middle trimmed first when over the cap; a long body paragraph's own links and emphasis are not credit lines; comments in the page are skipped.
- **Verifying the model.** A name counts only when it occurs verbatim, whitespace aside, inside a span that occurs verbatim in the credit text, as in X-004, and now only when that span also lies within one of the credit text's three parts (before the region, the region's short credit lines, after the region): the parts are cut separately, so a run across a seam joins text the page shows apart. This one-part rule applies to the model's title and date spans too. An author span that lies inside a single heading and nowhere else on the page is the title, not a credit, which extends the existing rule that a heading is never a credit line.
- **The headline.** A heading's math gives its TeX once, as the body's does, and a presentation twin (`aria-hidden`) is not collected again unless it holds the heading's only text. With no usable `<h1>`, the headline is the element before the region, outside page chrome, whose text holds the declared title with the least other text, then the innermost, then the one nearest the region: a headline element is mostly its headline, and a share widget that also holds the title is not. The region and the Markdown are unchanged (`markdown_sha` identical on all 90 development and check items).
- **Refactors** from two fresh-context reviews, each shown behaviour-preserving by replay (`--compare`, 0 differences): one locate, one verbatim check, one normaliser, entities unescaped once; dead parameters deleted. One change was not behaviour-preserving and was measured instead: a single declared-metadata reader replaced two, and a self-check that could not fail was deleted; it changed only the declared tier's candidates (its development titles right on 53 of 60 against 49), with every all-three figure unchanged. Also: each surviving constant commented, several only as a size bound or a policy choice (the credit text's cap, the `<time>` limit, the structure check's 0.9 threshold). The scorer was not touched.

**Known limits of the second freeze:**
- the author is right only where a source is configured: a byline outside its selector's element, a byline in a bare text node, a source with several templates, or a site redesign gives no author or a wrong one until the upstream step catches it; generality to a source nobody configured is not claimed;
- two selectors are fragile: hiddenlayer's rests on the element's position, calif's on styling classes;
- a list of joint authors inside the body (cisa) is reached by no selector;
- a self-citation that names people is not read (every self-citation here names the site, which is the publisher);
- "the site's own name" is recognised by equality only; a byline holding the site's name with other words is taken as an author;
- a source configured `null` (no byline element) that later starts printing bylines reports "no author" without notice until the upstream step's checks see it;
- a name in the page's metadata that the page never shows (a person, or an organisation other than the site) is still reported: declared metadata comes first and is not checked against the page;
- the publisher flag does not distinguish "the page credits its organisation" from "the page credits no one" in the authors field; the `byline` flag records it for the byline element only;
- agreement is not truth: on check S123 the declared date and the heuristic agree on a date the label rejects, and nothing flags it; the heuristic reads the same metadata first, so it is a fallback, never a voter;
- most dates rest on one source (71 of 90 on development and check), so a wrong declared or listing date passes as `single`;
- the date heuristic depends on the day of the run (its latest allowed date is today), so the same page could give a different fallback date if run before the page's own date;
- a date only the model could read is lost: dev S210, and any page like it;
- which sources may vote was set from these 90 items' disagreements; a new source may make a voter wrong, and a dispute is where that would first show;
- the targeted calls are unmeasured as deciders: escalation is advise-only, and the last resort never fired on development or check;
- the escalation call sees printed text only: a candidate that only metadata carries cannot be quoted, and in the trial the model answered "none" on 12 of 13 arXiv dates;
- a trailing site name outside the four recognised strings stays in a declared title and makes the title disputed, which is visible, not silent;
- a byline injected only by JavaScript on a page with no rendered copy, or a label printed inside the byline element, is not handled;
- the labels for joint lists disagree across sets: check S059 says "no one credited" and test S086 names 26 organisations; the stack gives no author on both;
- the reference and the second test must run on the same day: the date heuristic's candidate depends on the run date, and a difference would make the run invalid.

**Known limits of the first design, kept as the record:**
- the model decides whether a name is the page's credit, and nothing checks that judgement: on the 27 development and check pages with no credit it still credits someone on 3, the site's own name among them, and no mechanism measured here separated that from a real organisation credit (the second freeze removes this from the author decision);
- the model's answer moves with trivial changes to its input: about one model answer in seven, and one final author in fifteen, changed when the same content was presented differently;
- the model is asked for a title it usually cannot see, since headings are never credit lines; its title decides no development or check item;
- a byline between two long paragraphs, or printed as bare text with no element of its own, never reaches the model;
- a `<header>` outside the article and `main` is dropped as site chrome, with any byline it holds; on development and check it holds the title on one page and an author on none. A content region that itself sits inside such a header loses the text before and after it;
- the model's date is verified verbatim, but the prompt does not say to copy it as printed, so a date the model normalises is rejected; the model is still called when the credit text is empty;
- all 27 "no one credited" labels on development and check come from four sources, so the false-credit figures say little about other sources;
- a list of joint authors inside the body, after a lead-in paragraph (S086's 26 organisations), is outside the credit text;
- a quoted person whose attribution carries no quote markup can pass as an author, and so can a credit for another role ("Discovered by");
- the one-part rule follows the credit text's own seams, not the page's elements: a span joining two unrelated elements that both sit before the region (or both after it) still passes, and a real credit is rejected when its label lies outside the region and its names inside;
- the site's own name on a short line inside the region (check S150) passes both verification rules when the model credits it;
- the model sometimes names a credit but quotes a span that does not hold it; verification rejects the name, so the credit is lost rather than wrong;
- the heading rule rejects a byline the page marks up as a heading (an author card whose name is an `h3`, say); deleted in the second freeze;
- any element echoing the bare title outside page chrome (a breadcrumb not in a `nav`, a screen-reader-only copy) beats a visible "Prefix: Title" headline, which is the shape of the test's S207;
- the verbatim check matches within words, so a truncated name passes; the citation flip rests on the model's own kind;
- the joined-word structure check sees only ASCII words;
- a list mixing a person and an organisation keeps the organisation, as in X-004;
- an organisation is recognised partly by the site's host label, whose generic second-level domains are a hand-picked partial list rather than the Public Suffix List;
- a name in the page's metadata that the page never shows (a person, or an organisation other than the site itself) is still reported, and a declared person outranks the organisation the visible credit names; unseen on development and check, where all 14 declared people match the labels. A check that every name appears in the page's text was measured (2026-10-01): held to the credit text it turned 13 correct items wrong, held to the body text it cost 2 check items through formatting, and the owner kept the rule;
- the credit text's header has no link-farm filter, so a long list before the region can fill its share and push a byline out;
- the citation flip turns an organisation printed with one comma ("Example Lab, Inc.") into "Inc. Example Lab";
- the prompt's clause on organisations that "jointly released the item" invites organisation answers, which the verification rules then have to catch;
- the heuristic and trafilatura dates are capped at the day of the run;
- every source has a hand-found content selector (`sources.json`), so this tests known sources only.

**The first freeze (`fad09d5`), as it was.** The note, `criterion.json`, `tiers.json`, `label-corrections.json`, the pages manifest and all code are committed together in `design(X-005)`. `experiments/X-005/test_replay.py` refuses unless `experiments/X-005` (less `runs/` and `out/`) and the note are committed and unchanged at HEAD, with `criterion.json` and `tiers.json` equal to the `design(X-005)` commit's; refuses a second test, including when a test cache or a test manifest anywhere in git history exists; checks the totals (60 labels, 30 recent) before it starts; writes its manifest before the first model call, so a run that dies still counts against the budget; retries a failed model call once after a pause, and if the model is still unavailable stops with that status, the items not reached counted wrong and named, rather than scoring them without the model; reads the test labels from X-004's committed score manifest; and reports the corrected and as-submitted figures with the items fixed and broken against X-004.

**The second freeze, and a second test: a recorded deviation.** The redesign, `labels-reread.json` and a new `tiers.json` are committed together in a second `design(X-005)`.
