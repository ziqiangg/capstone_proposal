# X-001 Scraping feasibility across the Source Set

Answers [[Q-005]] on what each Source Set entry needs so that a Collection can collect it, and carries that question's priority, P1.

## Hypothesis

A collection stack that combines several tools, chosen per site (a plain fetch, a feed reader, a headless browser, and an extraction library, with a hosted crawling service where the others fail), fetches and keeps the raw page, and extracts structured text, for nearly every item BEACON's sources list, whatever countermeasures those sites apply ([[ACQ-01]], [[ACQ-02]], [[CLS-06]]).

## Setup

A trial list of English-language sources, recorded with this experiment until the initial Source Set ([[Q-005]]) is approved. Candidate tools, open source preferred; the combination per site is part of the result. During development every raw page is kept. Tool versions, the machine and the source list are written into each run's manifest. The trial list is frozen before the screen run. The Register, first shortlisted, was dropped at the freeze on the owner's ruling of 2026-09-28: its robots.txt denies AI and scraping crawlers, and a stack that honours robots.txt would record it as failing in every run. A site that forbids collection is a policy decision for the Source Set ([[Q-005]]), not a countermeasure the stack should defeat, so its absence does not flatter the coverage figure; any item another site's robots.txt refuses still counts as not kept.

The trial list is `experiments/X-001/sources.json`: each entry names the source, its address, how it lists its items (a feed, a listing page or an interface), why it is included, and what a Collection needs to collect it (the fetch modes to try in order, and the selector of the item's body). A source *fails entirely* in a run when its listing cannot be read, or when none of the items it lists has its raw page kept. The *item's body* is the page region the entry's selector names, less scripts, forms and the boilerplate the entry names; the scripted comparison counts each class of element there and in the extracted text. An item whose body holds under 200 characters fails the comparison, as a body the stack did not find. The metadata part compares the record with the raw page: title, author and Source publication time must be there wherever the page gives them, the time as the same instant, and every `<meta>` field, the canonical link, each JSON-LD block, the `<time>` values, the language and the title tag must be kept exactly as the page declares them. The record's text is the body region converted to Markdown: formulas are written as the LaTeX the page carries, tables Markdown cannot hold (merged cells, nested tables) stay as HTML, and tables that only lay out an equation are not tables. Beside it the record keeps two generic extractors' text, trafilatura's and defuddle's, which need no per-source selector, so the result can say what a selector buys. An item that is a PDF (an arXiv paper with no HTML version, a CSA bulletin) is kept as fetched and converted to Markdown by anydoc; it counts towards coverage, and stays out of the structure sample, which has no HTML body to compare it with. The headless-browser mode is real Chrome, run headful with its window off-screen: headless Chromium was refused by sites that served it. The stack honours each site's robots.txt, including the crawl delay it asks, and waits at least 2 seconds between requests to one host; an item that robots.txt refuses is not fetched and counts as not kept.

The structure clause was widened before any run, on the owner's ruling of 2026-09-28 ("keep as much structure as possible (all metadata, any page structure)"), to keep as much of each item as the page holds: every metadata field, and every class of element a later Stage may read ([[ACQ-02]] names the title, author and Source publication time, and keeping every other declared field too is a stricter test than it asks, not a change to it; [[CLS-06]] chunks by section, so it needs the headings). The window's floor of 3 is there because a source that publishes every few weeks would otherwise add nothing to the test.

The runs fall on one day, not on 7 consecutive days: each run backfills the 7 days a source lists, which tests coverage and extraction over a week of items, and the 7 runs spaced at least 30 minutes apart test intermittent failure and blocking on repeat visits. The spacing was 60 minutes until the owner's ruling of 2026-09-28, before any screen or full run: it only has to make each run a separate visit, production collects daily, and a shorter gap makes rate-based blocking more likely, not less; what it gives up is an outage that lasts between 30 and 60 minutes. This design cannot show a site that starts blocking only after days of daily visits, or a listing that changes from day to day. That is assumed away here; source failures in running ([[SRC-12]], [[SRC-14]]) reopen it.

## Criterion

Over 7 runs on one day, at least 30 minutes apart, across the trial sources, each run collecting every item a source lists as published in the preceding 7 days, or its 3 most recently listed items when that window holds fewer than 3: the stack fetches and keeps the raw page of at least 95 per cent of the items those sources list; no source fails entirely in more than 1 of the 7 runs; and on a sample of 100 of the items kept as HTML pages, at least 90 per cent keep their structure and metadata, meaning every class of element in the item's body (headings, lists, tables, code blocks, block quotes, formulas, links, and images with their captions) keeps at least 90 per cent of its elements in the extracted text, and the record holds the title, author and Source publication time wherever the page gives them, beside every metadata field the page declares, verbatim; judged by a scripted comparison with the raw page, which counts only if it agrees with a hand check on at least 9 of 10 of those items.

## Budget and staging

300 minutes of run time in all. Smoke: at most two minutes over three pages, to prove the script runs. Screen: one run over every trial source, to estimate a full run's time. Full: 7 runs on one day, at least 30 minutes apart. The script stops at the budget.

## Result

**Not met.** Two of the criterion's three parts pass; the third does not count.

Runs, all on 2026-09-28 (Asia/Singapore): smoke (10:53, 12:04), screen (12:07, 15.7 minutes, 175 of 175 items kept), and 7 full runs started 31 minutes apart from 12:23 to 15:29, each 15.7 minutes. 126 of the 300 budgeted minutes were used. Manifests: `experiments/X-001/runs/`.

1. **Raw page kept: met.** 1,235 of the 1,235 items the sources listed across the 7 runs (100 per cent, against 95).
2. **No source failing entirely in more than 1 run: met.** No source failed entirely in any run.
3. **Structure and metadata on 100 items: not met.** The scripted comparison passed 95 of the 100 sampled HTML items (the 4 PDF items kept were not sampled), but it agreed with the owner's hand check on 8 of 10, against 9 needed, so it does not count. The owner marked S077 (The Hacker News), S028 (Calif) and S015 (arXiv) as failing on metadata; the script passed S077 and S028. On the same 100 items trafilatura's text passed 20 and defuddle's 14.

What each source needed (the combination is part of the result):

| Source | Listing | Fetch mode that worked | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| METR blog | listing page | plain HTTP | low volume: floor of 3 |
| Calif research | listing page | plain HTTP | dates in plain text on the listing |
| The Hacker News | feed | plain HTTP | 49 items a week |
| The Straits Times, Tech | listing page, undated | plain HTTP | soft paywall markup, full text served |
| CNA, Business | feed | plain HTTP | a games widget dropped by selector |
| OpenAI news | feed | TLS impersonation | a plain client is refused (403) |
| CISA | legacy feed | TLS impersonation | a plain client is refused (403) |
| CSA (SingCERT) | listing page, with PDF bulletins | plain HTTP | its weekly bulletin needs OCR, which anydoc does not do; the raw PDF is kept |
| arXiv cs.CR | recent list, 50 newest | plain HTTP, 15-second crawl delay | HTML full text, else PDF, else abstract |
| HiddenLayer | listing page, undated | real Chrome (browser first, on purpose) | no site in the list needed JavaScript |

Why the third part fails, and what it shows:

- **Metadata by guess.** Where a page declares no author or date, the record took trafilatura's guess and labelled it as the page's. The guess outranked a listing date already in hand (Calif: 18 December 2024 recorded, 31 August 2026 listed and shown), took one author with an affiliation for a list of four (arXiv), and 140 of the 176 distinct HTML records hold a guessed date, 47 a guessed author. The scripted check compares only with declared metadata, so it cannot fail a wrong guess, and a visible byline the page does not declare (The Hacker News declares the site as author) is never checked. defuddle found the byline on The Hacker News and the author and date on Calif; arXiv's full metadata is on its abstract page, which the stack does not fetch when the HTML exists.
- **Two flaws in the scripted check**, found on the screen run and left as committed: it counts empty list items, which Markdown cannot write (all 3 OpenAI failures), and it leaves link titles in the text it matches captions against (arXiv caption failures). Both can only lower its pass rate.
- **The sample is small for the threshold.** 95 of 100 gives a 95 per cent interval of 88.8 to 97.8 per cent; over all 176 distinct HTML items the check passes 94.9 per cent (90.6 to 97.3).

What the design cannot show, as stated in the Setup: a site that starts blocking after days of visits, or a listing that changes from day to day. The Register was dropped at the freeze because its robots.txt denies crawlers; the 30-minute spacing cannot show an outage that lasts between 30 and 60 minutes.

The follow-up, on the owner's ruling of 2026-09-28, is a second experiment on extraction and metadata: metadata taken by provenance and labelled, every author, arXiv's metadata from its abstract page, a byline check against hand-labelled truth, the two check flaws fixed, and a sample large enough to settle the threshold.
