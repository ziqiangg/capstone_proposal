# CP-007 Onboarding and the Curator's part in it

## The change

Five replacements, fourteen new rows and two new parameters. Replacements: the Curator row of [[A-terms]] A1; the Source Set line of [[A-terms]] A2, replaced by itself and six new lines after it, Entry part, Onboarding, Onboarding outcome, Refusal ground, Tried and Standing; [[SRC-08]]'s requirement sentence and its rationale; [[SRC-01]]'s rationale. Fourteen new rows in group SRC, under the placeholders SRC-n01 to SRC-n14, each taking the next free number unless the owner assigns one; `apply` replaces each placeholder everywhere it appears in this change. Two new parameter notes, ONBOARDING_SAMPLE_SIZE, which SRC-n10 links, and BODY_PROSE_SHARE, which SRC-n11 links. Those links are written below under the placeholders `[[param-n1]]` (ONBOARDING_SAMPLE_SIZE) and `[[param-n2]]` (BODY_PROSE_SHARE), and `apply` replaces `param-n1` with `ONBOARDING_SAMPLE_SIZE` and `param-n2` with `BODY_PROSE_SHARE`, everywhere each appears in this change, in the same commit that adds the two parameter notes (`method/commit-grammar.md`, "Add a parameter and link it"). Every other line of [[A-terms]], [[SRC-01]] and [[SRC-08]] is unchanged, the frontmatter and **Verification** line of [[SRC-01]] and [[SRC-08]] included.

| Entry part | One thing a version of a source's Source Set entry holds for collecting the source or recording its items' fields, such as where its listing is, how its items' addresses are told from its other pages, the ways of fetching its pages and their order, where on an item's page its body, credited authors or printed date are found, or the source's name. |

**4. [[SRC-08]], the rationale, as it stands:**

```
**Rationale.** A source added automatically would change what BEACON reads without anyone deciding it.
```

**Replaced by:**

```
**Rationale.** A source added automatically would change what BEACON reads without anyone deciding it. A source added without an Onboarding would carry no Standing for any part of its entry and nothing its version gave before it was put into effect, so it would have no Drift reference and its drift could never be judged (A-terms, Drift reference). Entries first written by hand are no exception: their parts enter an Onboarding as a person's proposals and are checked like any other.
```

**5. [[SRC-01]], the rationale, as it stands:**

```
**Rationale.** A source that can be switched off only by deleting it loses its history. Adding, changing and removing a source are changes to a Governed Artefact, which the Curator makes through the means to change each Governed Artefact.
```

**Replaced by:**

```
**Rationale.** A source that can be switched off only by deleting it loses its history. Changing and removing a source are changes to a Governed Artefact, which the Curator makes through the means to change each Governed Artefact (GOV-17); adding one is a Curator's acceptance of an Onboarding outcome (SRC-08), which enables the source (SRC-n12). A Curator who wants an accepted source held back disables it here.
```

**6. New row SRC-n01** (giving a link), the full note:

```

type: "requirement"
group: "SRC"
verification: "none"
---
# SRC-n01

BEACON shall provide a Curator with the means to give BEACON the link to a source, starting an Onboarding of that source, recording the Curator, the time and the reason the Curator gives.

**Rationale.** Whether BEACON should read a source is the Curator's question, since the Curator owns the Source Set, so a Curator is who starts an Onboarding from a link; a Reader who knows of a source tells a Curator, who gives the link. Giving a link adds nothing to the Source Set: it starts the work whose outcome a Curator then accepts or does not (SRC-n07). The record says who asked for the source and why, so that the outcome can be read against the request.

# SRC-n03

**9. New row SRC-n04** (None offered for the body, the credited authors and the printed date), the full note:

```
---
id: "SRC-n04"
type: "requirement"
group: "SRC"
verification: "none"
---
# SRC-n04

BEACON shall give the Standing None offered to an Entry part locating an item's body, its credited authors or the date its page prints only where a Curator has confirmed that the source offers none (SRC-n03).

**Rationale.** A page that has no body, credits no one or prints no date is easily mistaken for a part that failed to find them, and a source wrongly taken to offer none of them would have every item recorded without them, with nothing to say so. That a source offers none of these three is therefore established only by a person looking at its pages; for other parts, what BEACON observes is enough. A page that later shows the thing contradicts the entry and raises a Drift flag (SRC-20).

**Verification.** none yet
```

**10. New row SRC-n05** (a name the same on every page), the full note:

```
---
id: "SRC-n05"
type: "requirement"
group: "SRC"
verification: "none"
---
# SRC-n05

BEACON shall take a name that a source's pages give alike at one place, on every page its Onboarding sampled that has it, as the site's own rather than as a credit to an Author only where those pages declare it as their publisher's or site's name or a Curator, shown it with the Onboarding outcome, confirms it.

**Rationale.** A name that is the same on every page may be the site's own, as a masthead is, or the one person who writes for it, as on a single-author blog. Taken as the site's own, the place that gives it is not read for credits, and the blog's author would be lost from every item. Sameness alone therefore does not make a name the site's: the pages must declare it as their publisher's or site's name, or a Curator, who can look at the site, must confirm it. Which credits a record keeps apart from its Authors is ACQ-02's.

# SRC-n08

**14. New row SRC-n09** (the ways of fetching), the full note:

```
---
id: "SRC-n09"
type: "requirement"
group: "SRC"
verification: "none"
---
# SRC-n09

WHEN BEACON collects a source, BEACON shall fetch its pages only in the ways of fetching that its Source Set entry in force names, trying them in the order it names them.

**Rationale.** Some sites refuse an ordinary request, and some ways of fetching get round that. Whether BEACON does so for a source is a decision about that source, so it is made in the entry a Curator accepted (SRC-n07) and never by BEACON on its own. An Onboarding establishes the ways and their order, and one that cannot is a refusal (A-terms, Onboarding outcome). A rise in refusals under a source's first way raises a Drift flag (SRC-16), so that the order is established and accepted again.

**15. New row SRC-n10** (the sample), the full note:

```

id: "SRC-n10"

verification: "none"

# SRC-n10

BEACON shall propose a version of a source's Source Set entry in an Onboarding only from a sample of [[param-n1]] of the source's items, spread across the periods from which the source lists items, or of every item the source lists where it lists fewer.

**Rationale.** An Entry part that holds on a few recent pages may fail on older ones made under an earlier layout, so the sample spreads across the source's periods. Its size weighs the pages each part is checked on against the pages fetched from a source no Curator has yet accepted. A source that lists fewer items is sampled whole rather than refused, and its outcome says how many items were sampled (A-terms, Onboarding outcome). A refused Onboarding is not bound: it may end before items can be fetched, or because the source disallows fetching them.

**16. New row SRC-n11** (the body's share of a page's prose), the full note:

```
---
id: "SRC-n11"
type: "requirement"
group: "SRC"
verification: "none"
---
# SRC-n11

BEACON shall propose in an Onboarding, as the Entry part locating a source's items' body, only a place on its pages that holds, on each page it is proposed from, at least [[param-n2]] of that page's varying prose, its text in blocks whose link density is low.

**Rationale.** An item's body is where most of its page's own prose is. Counting only varying text in blocks of low link density leaves out the site's template, the same on every page, and lets lists of other items barely count. A place holding less than this share is more likely a fragment of the article, or a sidebar, than its body. How link density is measured, which such place is proposed and how it is found are fixed by design. The re-checked version BEACON prepares after a Drift flag is no Onboarding, so this row does not bind it.

**18. New row SRC-n13** (an Onboarding ended by BEACON's own failure started again), the full note:

```
---
id: "SRC-n13"
type: "requirement"
group: "SRC"
verification: "none"
---
# SRC-n13

IF an Onboarding is ended by BEACON's own failure, at whatever point, THEN BEACON shall start that Onboarding again from the same link, a bounded number of times.

**Rationale.** A refusal rests on an Onboarding that finished looking (A-terms, Onboarding outcome). One that BEACON's own failure ended did not: a way that seemed to reach no page, or a listing not yet found, may be BEACON's own crash or lost connection. So it gives no outcome, nothing reaches a Curator as refused or proposed (SRC-n06), and grounds it observed are not made a refusal; a finished repeat observes them again. Most failures are transient, so it is started again, its repeats bounded as a Stage's retries are (OPS-14); the bound is fixed by design, no parameter covering an Onboarding.

**Verification.** none yet

**19. New row SRC-n14** (the Operator told when every repeat fails), the full note:

# SRC-n14

IF an Onboarding has been ended by BEACON's own failure and so has every repeat of it that SRC-n13 allows, THEN BEACON shall report that failure once, with the link and the reason, in the next Operator digest.

**Rationale.** BEACON's failures go to the Operator, who can see a crash or a lost connection and fix it, as a failed Re-extraction is reported to the Operator (ACQ-40); a Curator cannot. Without it the failure would be silent, and a defect that keeps recurring would stay hidden.

## Why

The owner's rulings on the [[Q-019]] forms (2026-10-04), the trim ruling of the same day
and the rulings of 2026-10-04 on the decisions the revised plans returned are the evidence,
with the owner's answers of 2026-10-06 on this proposal's draft choices and on its second, fourth, sixth and seventh assessments.
They do not answer [[Q-019]] or [[Q-026]]; they say how a source enters the Source Set and
what a Curator does in that, which no row as it stands says. [[SRC-07]] presents a
candidate, [[SRC-08]] adds a source only by a Curator's action and [[SRC-03]] holds what a
Collection needs, but nothing says how an entry is made from a link, what the Curator
confirms on the way, or what the Curator's action is. This is the sixth proposal of the
chain the trim ruling names (its item 19, "Curator capabilities"), after [[CP-002]] to
[[CP-006]]. It cites [[SRC-16]], [[SRC-18]] and [[SRC-20]], which [[CP-006]] added, and
[[ACQ-29]] and [[ACQ-23]], which earlier proposals added. Each paragraph below names the
item of the integrated plan's group 6 it carries, and the rulings it rests on.

**Giving a link (item 1; F2-23, F2-03).** F2-23 chose "(c) A Curator, plus discovery (seam
1), and no other role", with the note "The capability to give a link is granted to the
Curator; it needs a row ... Each onboarding records who or what started it. A Reader's
suggestion reaches a Curator, who gives the link." F2-03: "Giving a link starts onboarding
and is recorded, but it is not SRC-08's action." SRC-n01 grants the link, recording who,
when and why (plan 1's intake records "who gave it, when, and why"); SRC-n02 bounds what
starts an Onboarding to a Curator and BEACON's own search; the Onboarding outcome line
carries "who or what started it". Onboarding is defined from a link, so it is the Curator's
or the search's act alone.

**Confirming parts and "none offered" (item 2; F0-02, F2-01, F2-22).** F0-02: the person who
"confirms parts, marks values and establishes 'none offered'" is "(a) The Curator". F2-01:
"A first, then B for parts left open." F2-22: "'none offered' needs a witness that
positively observes the absence. For the byline, the date element and the body it is
established only by the person F0-02 names." SRC-n03 grants step B's answers; the Standing
line says what each answer makes of a part, with plan 1's four standings and its rule that
"cannot tell" changes nothing, and that "none" is offered only where BEACON found no value
(plan 1, 1.5). The Standing line holds of every Entry part an Onboarding considered, whatever its outcome, so a refusal for a part not established reads a Standing the part already has rather than one the refusal decides. SRC-n04 carries F2-22's restriction for the three parts, and the Standing line limits BEACON's own observation to the other parts, so that for those three what BEACON alone observed leaves the part Not established. Plan 1's written rule
for step B, its pages, and that a rejected part is not proposed again in the first version
(the ruling of 2026-10-04 on plan 1's G-5) are design and stay out of the rows.

**A constant name (item 3; F2-13).** F2-13: "Where a credit location is identical on every
page (a single-author site), the name is not taken as the site's own without a declared
publisher or site name, or a person's confirmation." The F4-07 note gives the reason: it
"protects single-author blogs", and its choice, "(a) No. Only the kept Item publisher is
taken out", leaves which credits a record keeps apart from its Authors to [[ACQ-02]] and
the Item publisher line. So SRC-n05 decides only whether an Onboarding takes such a name as
the site's own rather than as a credit, the Curator's confirmation within it, and changes
nothing a record keeps.

**Accepting an outcome, the ways of fetching included (item 4; F2-03, F2-02, F2-04, F2-05,
F3-13).** F2-03: "The Curator's action is accepting the outcome, shown with each part's
standing, and that same action puts the configuration into effect (F2-02)." SRC-n06 shows
the outcome with each part's Standing; SRC-n07 grants the acceptance. F2-04 chose "(e)
Established parts may be used checked or not; parts 'could not be established' for #1 or #2
refuse"; F2-05's note: "A refusal names something BEACON observed that makes collection
impossible or not allowed". The Onboarding outcome and Refusal ground lines carry both. F3-13 chose "(d) With
no configured order, the Collection does not start; onboarding must establish one", with
the note "the Curator accepts them, so getting around a site's bot protection is decided
per source and shown to the Curator, never automatic." SRC-n09 states that a Collection
fetches only in the ways, and the order, the accepted entry names. The no-order case is a
refusal, as the owner answered on 2026-10-06 (choice 1): "A refusal. As drafted: the outcome
is a refusal, which cannot be accepted; SRC-10 stays true. Matches your F3-13 'with no
configured order, the Collection does not start'. A source BEACON has no way to fetch can't be
collected, so accepting it would put an entry in the Source Set that can never run. That's a
silent dead source, exactly what SRC-04 exists to expose. It matches your F3-13 (d): no configured
order means no Collection, and onboarding must establish one. The refusal should name what was
observed, under F2-05's reasons. Every way being refused with 401/403 is most likely 'needs an
account', and its detail should say each way was refused." F2-05's note lists the reasons: "not
a public address, not reachable, collection disallowed, needs an account, no listing found".
The Refusal ground line names these five as BEACON's observations, "not a public address" and "not
reachable" apart, and gives each part whose not being established F2-04 (e) makes a refusal its
ground. The listing's is no listing found; the item address's is no listing found too, as
F2-04's note has it: "Where the item address cannot be established, the refusal is
