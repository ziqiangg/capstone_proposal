# CP-005 A field step or record write fails as a Stage does

## The change

Thirty-six replacements, five new rows and one new parameter. Replacements: the Stage, Complete cycle, Unfinished item, Failed item, Erased item, Yield and Revision lines of [[A-terms]] A2; [[OPS-14]]'s requirement sentence; [[OPS-03]]'s requirement sentence; [[REL-01]]'s, [[REL-08]]'s and [[FBK-06]]'s requirement sentences; [[REL-03]]'s rationale; [[ACQ-03]]'s requirement sentence and its rationale; [[ACQ-01]]'s rationale; [[ACQ-02]]'s requirement sentence; [[ACQ-22]]'s requirement sentence and its rationale; the rationales of [[ACQ-06]], [[ACQ-24]], [[ACQ-25]] and [[ACQ-35]]; [[ACQ-28]]'s and [[ACQ-29]]'s requirement sentences; [[ACQ-37]]'s requirement sentence and its rationale; [[GOV-06]]'s rationale; [[OPS-27]]'s requirement sentence and its rationale; [[OPS-28]]'s rationale; [[ITEM_RETRY_CYCLES]]'s `bounds` line; [[OPS-19]]'s requirement sentence and its rationale; [[ACQ-07]]'s requirement sentence; [[EXCLUDED_TEXT_RETENTION]]'s `bounds` line. Five new rows in group ACQ, under the placeholders ACQ-n01 to ACQ-n05, each taking the next free number unless the owner assigns one; `apply` replaces each placeholder everywhere it appears in this change. One new parameter note, LISTED_ITEM_RETRY_CYCLES, which ACQ-n02 links. That link is written below under the placeholder `[[param-n1]]`, and `apply` replaces `param-n1`, everywhere it appears in this change, with `LISTED_ITEM_RETRY_CYCLES`, in the same commit that adds the parameter note: `vault_check` P1 refuses, in any note, a link to a parameter note that does not exist yet, so the proposal cannot carry the link itself (see the notes, "The parameter's link"). Every other line of the rows, annex and parameter named is unchanged: their frontmatter other than the `bounds` lines of [[ITEM_RETRY_CYCLES]] and [[EXCLUDED_TEXT_RETENTION]], the requirement sentences of [[ACQ-01]], [[ACQ-06]], [[GOV-06]], [[ACQ-24]], [[ACQ-25]], [[ACQ-35]], [[REL-03]] and [[OPS-28]], the rationales of [[ACQ-02]], [[REL-01]], [[REL-08]], [[FBK-06]], [[OPS-14]], [[OPS-03]], [[ACQ-07]], [[ACQ-28]] and [[ACQ-29]], every **Verification** line, the headings of [[ITEM_RETRY_CYCLES]] and [[EXCLUDED_TEXT_RETENTION]], the latter's "Its bounds name" line, and every other line of [[A-terms]].

## Why

The owner's rulings on the [[Q-019]] forms (2026-10-04), the trim ruling of the same day,
the ruling of 2026-10-05 naming the new parameter, the rulings of 2026-10-05 on this
draft and the rulings of 2026-10-05 on its first and second assessments are the evidence. They do not
answer [[Q-019]] or [[Q-026]]; they say how a field step, a record write or a
Re-extraction that fails is handled, what a later return does to what an earlier one left
unrecorded, how often an item a source lists but BEACON cannot fetch is tried again, and
how an erased item's unrecorded return ends, which [[A-terms]], [[OPS-14]], [[ACQ-01]],
[[ACQ-03]], [[ACQ-28]], [[ACQ-29]] and [[ACQ-37]] as they stand cannot say. A Stage is
"one of the named analysis steps an admitted item passes through", and a Failed item is
"an Admitted item"; an item whose fields or record fail has no record and so no scope
decision, so it is neither, and no failure policy reaches it. This is the fourth proposal
of the chain those rulings name, after [[CP-002]], [[CP-003]] and [[CP-004]]. Each
paragraph below names the item of the integrated plan's group 4, or the ruling on this
draft, it carries.

**Stage and Failed item widened (item 1; F5-02).** F5-02: "(a), using the existing failure
policy. With F4-01 (a) and F1-15, a working field step always delivers a field set, so an
article with text and no record means a crash or a defect. What arrived is held as pending
and never served. It is a breach of ACQ-02, REL-01 and FBK-06, shown in the Operator view
and digest (OPS-07, OPS-23). (c) is excluded: the content arrived, so the article is a
Discovered item (ACQ-01). The field step and the record write fail as Stages do", retried
"up to STAGE_RETRY_LIMIT in the Cycle (OPS-14)", "resumed or retried by the Operator
(OPS-05, OPS-10)", "carried into later Cycles and dropped after ITEM_RETRY_CYCLES with its
reasons, the Operator told once (OPS-19, OPS-27, OPS-28)" and "counted per source (SRC-12 to
SRC-15)"; "One proposal widens Stage and Failed item to cover the field step and the record
write". The Stage line now names the field step and the record write, by what each does,
beside the analysis steps; a Failed item is now a Discovered item, of which an Admitted
item is one, so every row that reads Stage or Failed item reaches the two new steps
unchanged: [[EVD-10]] records the failure and its reason, [[OPS-04]] lets other work go on,
[[OPS-05]] and [[OPS-10]] retry it, [[OPS-07]] and [[OPS-23]] show it, [[OPS-19]] carries
it, [[OPS-27]] and [[OPS-28]] drop it and tell the Operator once, and [[SRC-12]] to
[[SRC-15]], with [[OPS-21]], count it. "Fetch failures stay under F3-03": the field step
works on what a Collection returned, so a fetch that returns nothing is not a Stage.

**A failed Re-extraction (ruling on this draft, "Part of the record write").** The owner
ruled: "Part of the record write. A returning item's Re-extraction and comparison (ACQ-30)
are part of its record write, so their failure is a Failed item under the policy", citing
the owner's own words of 2026-10-05, "A comparison that fails for another reason is a
failure handled by group 4's failure policy". The Stage line's record write now includes
the Re-extraction [[ACQ-30]] makes for a return and the comparison under [[ACQ-06]], and
says no other Re-extraction is part of a Stage. The rationales of [[GOV-06]] and [[ACQ-37]]
called that Re-extraction "part of that item's Collection" and "part of its Collection";
a Collection is "BEACON fetching what one source's Source Set entry returns", and the record
write is a Stage after it ([[ACQ-03]]), so both now call it part of the record write of
that return, a Stage after its Collection. The owner added: "a Re-extraction in a run
(a release, an accepted correction, an Operator command) that fails is reported to the
Operator with its reason and is not counted against the source (SRC-12). The item stays on
its current version, which is then not current, so it is re-extracted when it returns
(ACQ-n03) or by the Operator's command. The source's Collection is not held back by it, and
ACQ-n01 is met for the items whose Re-extraction succeeded." (The ruling's ACQ-n03 and
ACQ-n01 are [[CP-004]]'s placeholders, now [[ACQ-30]] and [[ACQ-28]].) ACQ-n03 holds the one
obligation in it, the report; the rest follows from rows, as ACQ-n03's rationale says: a
run's Re-extraction is not part of a Stage, so it makes no Failed item and [[SRC-12]] does
not count it; a failure gives no result, so [[ACQ-09]] and [[ACQ-33]] record nothing and
the item keeps its latest version, which [[ACQ-30]]'s test then finds not current where
that version was made by earlier procedures or under another entry. An Operator's command
can name an item already current, which a failure leaves current, so the rationale says
"where". [[ACQ-28]] as it stood required a Re-extraction of every item in reach before the
source's first Collection, so a failure would either hold the Collection back or breach the
row; it now excepts an item whose Re-extraction fails, which meets the ruling's last
sentence. [[ACQ-29]] has the same deadline ("before that source's next Collection") and
takes the same exception. [[ACQ-37]] counted a run's versions once its Re-extractions "have
all been made", which a run with a failure would never meet; it now reads "have each been
made or have failed". [[ACQ-31]] sets no deadline and stays true.

**The knock-ons (item 2; F5-02, the final review's list).** Each row and term that speaks
of an Admitted item, or is defined over one, was read against the widening:
- [[OPS-14]] retries a failed Stage "for an Admitted item"; an item whose field step fails
  is not one, so F5-02's first bullet would be unmet. It now reads "a Discovered item".
- [[OPS-03]] recorded the Stage reached and its state "for each Admitted item". [[OPS-07]]
  shows "each Stage's state", and [[OPS-05]] and [[OPS-19]] resume at the Stage an item had
  reached, so for an item still in its field step or record write when a Cycle is
  interrupted or closed no row would record that Stage or its state. It now reads "for
  each Discovered item", as [[OPS-14]] does, other than an Erased item that BEACON does not
  hold, of which nothing is recorded (below, "The knock-ons of the widened Erased item").
- *Unfinished item* was "An Admitted item of a Cycle that has not reached the final Stage".
  An item whose field step or record write is still running when the Cycle would close is
  not admitted, so [[OPS-16]] would close the Cycle without it and [[OPS-19]] would not
  carry it. It now also covers a Discovered item whose record write has not completed. The
  record write is every Discovered item's: a new item's record ([[ACQ-02]]), a held item's
  Encounter ([[ACQ-05]]) and any Revision ([[ACQ-06]], [[ACQ-27]]); so "has not completed"
  is defined for every Discovered item. An item recorded but not yet given a scope decision
  is neither, as today: the scope decision is not a Stage, and the widening does not change
  that.
- *Complete cycle* required only that "every admitted item reached the final stage", so a
  Cycle in which a record write failed would be complete. It now also requires every
  discovered item's record write to have completed.
- [[OPS-19]] speaks of Unfinished and Failed items and "the Stage it had reached"; with both
  terms widened it carries a pending item to the next Cycle and resumes it at its field step
  or record write, so the widening alone leaves it true; it changes only for a dropped
  item's return (below, "The last two choices").
- [[ACQ-03]] binds "every Stage after Collection" to work "from the item's retained Article
  Record". The record write writes that record, and the field step precedes it, so neither
  could meet the row. F5-02 rules them "retried from the kept material without fetching"
  (its first bullet); [[ACQ-03]] now names what BEACON keeps of the Collection's return as
  what those two also read, still without fetching, and its rationale cites [[ACQ-17]],
  which already reaches "any extracted text of the item that BEACON holds but has not yet
  recorded in a version". The record-write's Re-extraction ([[ACQ-30]]) still reads the
  retained Article Record, which the row keeps.
- [[SRC-12]] counts "items that have become Failed items". With the widening the count
  includes items whose field step or record write failed, a returning item's Re-extraction
  or comparison included, as F5-02's last bullet rules ("counted per source (SRC-12 to
  SRC-15)"). Its sentence and rationale stay true: each item is counted once, however many
  Cycles it fails in; an item a source lists but BEACON cannot fetch makes no Failed item by
  that, a run's failed Re-extraction makes no Failed item, and an erasure ends an unrecorded
  return without one (ACQ-n04, ACQ-n05), so none of them is counted.

**A later return replaces what is kept (ruling on this draft, "Add both rows").** The owner
ruled: "Add both rows. One row each, one obligation each. A rationale states no obligation,
and an unwritten replacement rule would be untestable.- The replacement rule is your F5-02
ruling: a later Collection's arrival replaces the pending material." F5-02: "A later
Collection's arrival replaces pending material." ACQ-n01 says so. "Pending material" is
written as what BEACON keeps of an earlier return but has not yet recorded in the item's
Article Record, the words [[ACQ-17]] already uses for its text, so no new term is needed.

**The bound across Cycles (item 3; F3-03, trim item 12, the rulings of 2026-10-05).**
F3-03: "(b), and a bound across Cycles as its own Operator-owned parameter (not
ITEM_RETRY_CYCLES): after that many Cycles a listed item that still cannot be fetched is no
longer retried and is reported as not fetched". The trim accepted item 12 as "F3-03: retries
within a Collection and the pause are constants; the bound across Cycles stays an Operator
parameter", so only the bound is a parameter. The integrated plan's ruled default is "3",
and on 2026-10-05 the owner named it: "LISTED_ITEM_RETRY_CYCLES" (Cycles a listed article
whose content cannot be fetched is tried again; default 3; distinct from
ITEM_RETRY_CYCLES). On this draft the owner ruled the bound a row ("A rationale explains; it
doesn't oblige. A retry bound that lives only in ACQ-01's rationale binds nothing"):
ACQ-n02 states it and links the parameter, and [[ACQ-01]]'s rationale, which said "the next
Cycle tries it again", now cites ACQ-n02. F3-03 speaks of "a listed item that still cannot
be fetched", not only of one never fetched, so ACQ-n02 counts an unbroken run of Cycles in
which BEACON tried and fetched nothing, from the first of them on: it reaches a held item
whose page a later paywall or block makes unreachable as it reaches an item never fetched,
and "the first of those Cycles" is fixed by the run, not by when BEACON first tried the item
ever. [[ACQ-01]]'s rationale said such an item "is not yet a Discovered item", which is
false of a held item; it now says the Collection runs no Stage for it and makes no Failed
item of it. The parameter's lower limit: "(2) A minimum of 1, like ITEM_RETRY_CYCLES. Zero
would mean an unetched [sic] article is never tried again, which contradicts ACQ-01." Its
`bounds` count the Cycles that try it *again*, as the owner's wording says, so the default
of 3 tries it in the first Cycle of the run and in up to three more, which ACQ-n02's "in one
Cycle and in [[param-n1]] later Cycles" matches.

**The smaller choices (ruling on this draft, "Accept all three").** "(1) No extra row for an
Erased item's pending material: ACQ-17 and ACQ-18 already remove what was kept", "(2)

reported: "Next Operator digest. Each failed item, its source and the reason go into the

shows it", and "OPS-28 notifies "once for that item", and the item keeps the same identity
when it comes back, so a repeated drop doesn't alert the Operator again." No row is added
to keep such a return out. [[OPS-27]] as it stood counted every Cycle in which an item had
been a Failed item, so a returned item would be dropped again at its first failure, not
"retried at most ITEM_RETRY_CYCLES each time it comes back"; it now leaves out every Cycle
before the one in which a Collection first returned the item after BEACON last dropped it.
The first such return, not the latest: an item its source still lists is returned in every
Cycle, so a count restarted at the latest return would count one Cycle at most and the item
would never be dropped again. Counted from the first, it is dropped again after
[[ITEM_RETRY_CYCLES]] more Cycles in which it fails, and comes back with the next return,
which is "each time it comes back" and "only while the listing still shows it".
[[ITEM_RETRY_CYCLES]]'s `bounds` described the item's total ("the number of Cycles in which
an item may be a Failed item before BEACON drops it") and now count the same Cycles as
[[OPS-27]]. [[OPS-27]]'s rationale says the item keeps its identity, as [[OPS-28]]'s "once for that
item" reads it. [[OPS-19]] carried only Failed items "that BEACON has not dropped", which a
returned item that fails again never is, so the restarted count could not run; it now
carries each Failed item of the closed Cycle "that BEACON has not dropped since that Cycle
began". It reads the drop as an event, as the text at HEAD did, not the Dropped item state
that [[OPS-19]] itself decides: an item dropped in the closed Cycle is not carried, and an
item dropped earlier that a Collection returned and that failed again in it is. Scoping the
drop to the closed Cycle, not to "no Collection has returned it since", keeps the outcome
from depending on whether the next Cycle's Collection has yet run. [[ACQ-07]] ran its time
"since an item was dropped", so an earlier drop would erase the text of a returned item
that BEACON processes again; it now runs from the latest drop and stops only when a later
return is processed again, as the second assessment's ruling says (below, "ACQ-07's
clock").

**An erased item's unrecorded return (ruling on the first assessment, "Ends at its
Encounter").** The first assessment found that an item a Curator erases while BEACON keeps
a return of it not yet recorded would fail its field step, with nothing left to read
([[ACQ-17]], [[ACQ-18]]), until it was dropped, counted against its source and alerted to

act, not a source failure.- It's my F5-03 wording, which you accepted. An erased article "is

the wrong party. The failures happen because a Curator erased the article, not because the
source misbehaved. Counting them against the source (SRC-12) would push up its failure
count and might trigger a per-source alert. The Operator would be alerted to drop an article
that was deliberately removed. Both are false signals.- It matches how an erased article's
return is already handled. ACQ-22 records the return of an erased article as an Encounter

the erasure (ACQ-17, ACQ-18), and the Encounter records that the source still listed it."
ACQ-n04 is that one row for an item BEACON holds: the return's record write is completed
with its Encounter alone, and the Stage line gives an Erased item no field step, so no Stage
is left to retry for it; the erasure makes no Failed item, so [[SRC-12]] counts nothing for

**An erased item BEACON does not hold (ruling on the second assessment, "Nothing recorded,

recorded, persists. The pending return ends with nothing recorded: no record, no Failed

must hold whether or not BEACON had finished recording the article. The request is about the
content, not about BEACON's internal state. Under option 2, the article would simply be
collected again at its next return, which is the "takedown that doesn't stick" problem the
final review caught for F5-03.- The erasure's own record is the trace. Who erased it, when

**ACQ-07's clock (ruling on the second assessment, "Stop only if processed again").** The
second assessment found that [[ACQ-07]], as the first rework wrote it, stopped a dropped
item's clock at any later return, so a held item dropped at a later Stage that came back
unchanged, and that no Stage processes again, would never have its text erased. The owner
ruled: "Stop only if processed again. The clock runs from the latest drop and stops only
when a later return of the item is processed again (its field step or a Stage runs for it).
An unchanged return that no Stage processes leaves the clock running. My recommendation: it
is what your 'fresh start' was for.- ACQ-07 exists to drop text nobody uses. An article
dropped at a later Stage that comes back unchanged isn't processed again (ACQ-04), so its
text is still unused, and the clock should keep running. Stopping it at any return would
keep that text forever, against ACQ-07's purpose.- If the return is processed again, because
the field step or a Stage runs for it (your "fresh start"), the text is in use again and the
clock rightly stops.- As the question says, this only matters if a Curator turns ACQ-07 on,
since it's off by default. The rule should still be right." [[ACQ-07]] now runs from the
latest drop of an item "of which BEACON has since processed no return other than one
recorded as an Encounter with unchanged extracted text (ACQ-04)". That return is the
ruling's "unchanged return that no Stage processes", which "isn't processed again (ACQ-04)":
its field step and record write run only to record the Encounter, so the row names the
outcome, not the steps. Every other return of the item is processed again and stops the
clock: a new item's record, a Revision, or a return whose field step or record write runs
and fails, which makes the item a Failed item again, so that a later drop starts the clock
again. [[EXCLUDED_TEXT_RETENTION]]'s `bounds` spoke of the text "of a dropped item"; they
now say "of a dropped item that BEACON has not processed again since its latest drop
(ACQ-07)".

**The knock-ons of the widened Erased item (third assessment; follows "Nothing recorded,
persists").** Before this change an Erased item was a Held item, so no row that reads "a
Discovered item it does not already hold" ever reached one. Now one can be: an item erased
while BEACON kept its return unrecorded, and each later return of it until a lift. The
ruling gives it nothing ("no record, no Failed item, no SRC-12 count, no alert"), so each
row that would require something of it is narrowed: [[REL-01]] records no scope decision for
it, [[REL-08]] no OUT for its language, [[FBK-06]] begins no forward processing of it,
[[OPS-03]] records no Stage or state for it, and the Yield line no longer counts its return
as yield. [[SRC-02]], [[SRC-05]] and [[SRC-06]] read yield and so read correctly unchanged:
a source's yield counts each return of an item BEACON does not hold and that is not an Erased item, an item never held whose field step or record write fails included, and a source that returns
only Erased items yields nothing and is reported as silent, which is true of what BEACON
keeps from it. After a lift of an item BEACON never held, its first kept version is a new

Curator lifted the erasure of an item BEACON holds", matching [[ACQ-27]], which presupposes

**Rationales the change makes untrue (fourth assessment; follows the rulings above).** Four
