**5. New row SRC-n01** (a Drift flag is raised), the full note:

```
---
id: "SRC-n01"
type: "requirement"
group: "SRC"
verification: "none"
---
# SRC-n01

WHEN a Cycle closes, BEACON shall raise, for each source enabled both when that Cycle started and when it closes, a Drift flag on the version of the source's Source Set entry then in force, for each thing a Drift flag may name, other than by one page contradicting it or one Collection meeting it (SRC-n05, SRC-n06), in which what BEACON read, recorded or met over that version's Drift window for that thing ([[param-n1]]) departs from its Drift reference by more than chance alone would give with a probability of [[param-n2]], unless a Drift flag on that version for that thing is open.

**Rationale.** A source's pages change without notice: a redesign moves the byline, a bot block serves a page with no article on it. Collections still complete, so no failure is recorded (SRC-12), yet what BEACON reads is wrong. Each version is judged against its own Drift reference, of which how often something the entry holds finds nothing is part, not a departure in itself; judged as a rate, an occasional page with no article on it raises no flag where a rise does. The judgement runs when a Cycle closes (OPS-16), since a flag reaches the Curator only through the daily digest (A-terms, Digest), and leaves out a source disabled by then, disabling being already an answer to a flag (SRC-01). Collections and records go on under a flagged version, each record naming its version (ACQ-36). How a departure is judged is fixed by design.

**Verification.** none yet
```

**6. New row SRC-n02** (an open Drift flag in each Curator digest), the full note:

```

id: "SRC-n02"
type: "requirement"
group: "SRC"
verification: "none"
---
# SRC-n02

BEACON shall add each open Drift flag to each Curator digest sent while that flag is open, naming the source, the version of its Source Set entry and the thing the flag names, with what BEACON saw, how many of the source's items that are not Erased items have their latest raw page collected under that version, which a version correcting it would reach (ACQ-29), and how many of the source's items whose Article Record keeps no raw page have their latest version recorded as made under that version (ACQ-36), which it would not.

**Rationale.** A flag needs a Curator's decision, since the Curator owns the Source Set: disable the source (SRC-01), accept a version of its entry correcting the flagged one (ACQ-29), or keep the flagged version with a reason (SRC-n03); the flag is open until one of them, or until nothing is left for it to act on (A-terms, Open (Drift flag)). A silence reports itself once (SRC-06), but an open flag means records are still being made under a version that may be reading the source wrongly, so it is repeated in each digest until it is decided. The two counts say what a correction would still reach; raw pages are kept until an erasure under ACQ-08 removes them (ACQ-02, ACQ-18), so the second count is normally zero. A Drift flag is not a Failed item, so it adds nothing to the source's count (SRC-12) and alerts no Operator.

**Verification.** none yet
```

**7. New row SRC-n03** (keeping a flagged version), the full note:

```
---
id: "SRC-n03"
type: "requirement"

verification: "none"
---
# SRC-n03

BEACON shall provide a Curator with the means to keep a version of a source's Source Set entry despite what an open Drift flag on it names, recording the Curator, the time and the reason.

**Rationale.** A Curator may judge that what a flag shows is the source's own change and not a fault of its entry, such as a source that has stopped naming its authors. Keeping the version is a decision, as disabling the source or correcting its entry is, so it is made explicitly and leaves its reason on record, as a keep of a failing source does (SRC-15); without it a flag the Curator does not act on stays open. A keep closes the flag (A-terms, Open (Drift flag)), and from then on that version is judged for what the flag named only on what came since the keep (SRC-n01; A-terms, Drift window, Drift reference).
```

**8. New row SRC-n04** (the Health entry in the Curator digest), the full note:

```
---
id: "SRC-n04"
type: "requirement"
group: "SRC"
verification: "none"
---
# SRC-n04

Each Curator digest shall report, for each Cycle closed since the previous digest, the Health entry of each source enabled when that Cycle started.

**Rationale.** A source decays in ways that record no failure: fewer new items, pages that cannot be fetched, titles that have to be supplied, fields that can no longer be read. Each count in the Health entry is one such sign, given per source and per Cycle, so that the Curator, who owns the Source Set, sees a source weaken before it falls silent (SRC-06), fails (SRC-14) or is flagged (SRC-n01, SRC-n05, SRC-n06). A rise in fields held with no kept value is often the first sign that a source's pages have changed shape; one held so because what was found does not meet the rows (ACQ-12) is counted too, under its reason, so that the Curator's picture of the source is whole. The sources enabled when a Cycle starts are those it collects (SRC-10).

**Verification.** none yet
```

```
# SRC-n05
```

## Why

**This form: rows of a functional requirement's length (2026-10-05).** The seventh
assessment found the sixth form coherent. The owner then found that "length of items in
CP-006.md has grown longer than what a typical functional requirement should be" and
ruled, on 2026-10-05, "Restructure first": restructure before ruling on the proposal. This
form changes no content; only its form changes, as [[CP-002]]'s did. Twenty gaps had each
been closed by a clause inline, so SRC-n01's requirement had grown to 372 words and its
rationale to 1,159, and the Drift flag and Health entry lines to more than 360 each,
against rows such as [[SRC-01]], [[SRC-12]] and [[ACQ-29]] of one sentence and a short
reason. What a thing is judged on is now the A2 term Drift window, what it is judged
against the term Drift reference, when a flag is open the term Open (Drift flag), and the
versions a Health entry counts the term Counted version. SRC-n01 cites the window and the
reference and SRC-n05 the window; DRIFT_WINDOW's and DRIFT_ALPHA's bounds cite the terms
rather than restating them. Each rationale keeps its reason, and the reasoning it gave for
particular cases is kept in the notes, where the case list names the terms and rows that
now give each outcome, each the outcome it had. Where the paragraphs below quote wording
SRC-n01 or a line held, they name the term that now holds it.

The owner's rulings on the [[Q-019]] forms (2026-10-04), the trim ruling of the same day
and the rulings of 2026-10-05 on this group are the evidence. They do not answer
[[Q-019]] or [[Q-026]]; they say how a Curator learns that a source's entry has stopped
reading the source as it did, and what per-source counts the Curator sees, which no row as
it stands says. This is the fifth proposal of the chain the trim ruling names (its item 19,
"the Curator digest"), after [[CP-002]] to [[CP-005]], and it comes before the Curator
capabilities proposal (group 6), which cites rows this one adds. Each paragraph below names
the item of the integrated plan's group 5 it carries.

**The drift flag (item 1; F2-19, F2-16, F1-21, F2-24).** F2-19 chose "(b) A new row by
proposal: a drift flag goes once to the next Curator digest, as SRC-06 does for silence",
with the note: "A drift flag appears in the Curator digest, naming the source, the part,
what was seen and how many affected items can still be corrected, and it appears again in
each digest until the Curator acts. The Curator disables the source (SRC-01), accepts a
re-checked version BEACON prepared (F2-02), or keeps the configuration with a reason,
recorded as SRC-15 records a keep." "Goes once" and "appears again in each digest" are read
together as the integrated plan reads them: a flag is raised once for a version and a thing
while it is open (the "unless" clauses of SRC-n01, SRC-n05 and SRC-n06), and reported in every digest until a Curator
decides it (SRC-n02). F2-02 ruled that "the configuration is the Source Set entry: what each
part holds is versioned, and every version takes effect by a Curator's action", so a flag
is on a version of a Source Set entry, the form [[ACQ-29]], [[ACQ-32]] and [[ACQ-36]]
already use. What the entry holds is called "something the entry holds for reading the
source's pages", not "part", which the vault does not define. F2-16 ruled a rolling window
of 20: "D2 and D3 judge over at most a source's last 20 items under one configuration
version, and may flag after any Collection once the evidence is decisive at F2-17's level,
without waiting for the window to fill"; the Drift window holds "at most
[[param-n1]]" of what a thing is judged on, for most things "the source's items collected whose
record write has completed", and the notes say the rest; the
other units it names are the ruling on the third assessment's (below). When the judgement
runs is the owner's ruling on the second assessment (below): once per Cycle, at its close. A
source has one Collection in a Cycle, so "after any Collection" is read as at the close of
any Cycle that started with the source enabled (the ruling on the third assessment) and closes
with it enabled (the ruling on the fourth draft). F1-21 ruled that
"what it yielded" crosses for every item, so the shape of what something finds is judged
(D4), and that "each part's 'matched nothing' rate is part of its D2 baseline, not drift in
itself", which SRC-n01's rationale says. F2-24 ruled that the hand entries "enter as a
person's proposals and are checked like any other", so every version, the first included,
gave results before it was put into effect; no row requires that check, so the Drift
reference line and SRC-n06 name only "what that version gave before it was
put into effect", SRC-n01 and DRIFT_ALPHA's bounds cite that line, and none says how it was checked. The Drift flag line of A-terms lists what a flag may
name, by the four signals the plan sets out, worded as behaviour: a contradiction (D1), the
rates of something the entry holds (D2), the field profile, "gives none, supplied,
disagreeing" (D3, F1-12, F4-03), and the shape of what it finds (D4). The test itself is
design and is not named.

**The fetch-level events as triggers (item 1; F3-01, F3-04, F3-10, F3-13, trim item 14,
the ruling on the third assessment).** F3-01: "Many such events in one Collection mean the
source changed its address scheme: that raises a drift flag (F2-19)". F3-04: "A source whose
pages fail this test in two Collections running raises a drift flag (likely blocking)". F3-10
as the trim amended it: "A listing with zero entries, on a source onboarding never saw empty,
is a D2 event on the listing part." F3-13: "A source whose first mode is refused on most
articles raises a drift flag through D2, and its order is re-established and accepted
again." Trim item 14 routed "F3-01's 'many', F3-04's and F3-10's 'two running'" "through D2
at alpha". The owner's ruling on the third assessment (below) sorts these by kind, and the
Drift flag line names each as it sorts them. An address that is already another held item's
belongs to an item that is returned and recorded, so it is judged as before, by how often,
over the items whose record write has completed. F3-04's "this test", a page "answered with
success that shows none of" the source's "constant template locations", is worded as "a page
that shows none of what the entry holds as appearing on every page of the source". That page
and a refusal under the first way of fetching (F3-13) are the ruling's page-level events:
such a page "never becomes a Raw page, a record or a Revision" (F3-04), and an item refused
in every way is not returned ([[ACQ-01]]), so SRC-n01 judges how often they occur over the
Drift window's listed items tried, "the
items the source listed whose content a Collection tried to fetch", which is the ruling's
"listed articles the source attempted" in the words the Health entry line and [[ACQ-39]]
already use. An item tried in more than one Collection ([[ACQ-39]]) is counted once, by the outcome of
its latest try (the ruling on the fourth draft, below), so an item refused at each try is one
event, not several. The empty listing and every
listed item refused are the ruling's whole-Collection events, which SRC-n06 flags on their
first occurrence. "On a source onboarding never saw empty" is worded as "where no listing read
under that version before it was put into effect had none", the reference F2-24 gives every
version; a source whose listing was empty then raises no flag for an empty one, as F3-10
amended and the ruling both have it. "Every way of fetching refused for every listed article"

tried": an item BEACON did not try to fetch, such as one [[ACQ-39]] no longer tries, was not refused. After a keep for either,
SRC-n01 judges how often the source's Collections meet it over its Collections since the
keep. One window serves each unit: F2-16's window is D2's, the ruling judges the page-level
events "by D2", and F3-10 amended makes the empty listing "a D2 event", so DRIFT_WINDOW counts
the listed items tried or the Collections where elsewhere it counts the items recorded, and
its unit says so. Trim item 14's "no arbitrary count threshold" holds, as the ruling says:
F3-04's and F3-10's "two running" is gone, each rate being judged at DRIFT_ALPHA's level. A
contradiction raises a flag on one item: the trim made F2-18's "a contradiction is an
observation, not a rate; one raises a flag" a constant (trim item 13), so no parameter is
named for it; SRC-n05 carries it, in the Drift window SRC-n01 judges most things over.

**The three responses and the counts (item 1; F2-19, F2-20, F0-03).** The flag is open

re-extracts the items collected under the drifted version". Keeping is SRC-n03, worded as
[[SRC-15]] is, since F2-19 has the keep "recorded as SRC-15 records a keep". F2-20 also
rules "Records go on, each traceable to its configuration version", which [[ACQ-36]]
already requires; SRC-n01's rationale says so, and no row holds Collections back on a flag.
F2-19 counts "how many affected items can still be corrected"; F0-03's note, as the
retention ruling revised it, speaks of "the flag's count of items that cannot be corrected
