# Q-019 How BEACON must extract an item's text and metadata

## What is unknown

How must BEACON extract an item's text and metadata so that the record holds what the page shows: every structure class, and the title, every author and the Source publication time ([[ACQ-02]]), each labelled with where it came from? Keeping every author and labelling each field's provenance are design choices this question adds; [[ACQ-02]] asks only for the author and Source publication time wherever the source gives them. [[X-001]] kept every raw page its sources listed, but where a page declares no author or date its stack took a generic extractor's guess and labelled it as the page's: a guessed date outranked a listed one, one author and an affiliation stood for four, and a site name stood for a visible byline. Which tool, in which order, is right for each field is what the Extractor would be built on.

## Why this priority

P1: the Extractor is designed on the answer, and that design is the next collection work. The work can proceed meanwhile on X-001's region extractor, with undeclared metadata left empty, so it is not P0; it is not P2, because until it is answered every record leaves empty the undeclared fields the owner's hand check found missing on X-001's pages: bylines, full author lists and dates shown only in the page or the listing.

## Answered when

The experiment that follows X-001 on this question is recorded, and the owner approves an extraction design that names, for each of title, authors and Source publication time, the order of the tiers it is taken from, supported by that experiment's measured rate at which each tier's value matched the owner's hand labels (titles alike after case, whitespace and a trailing site name are set aside; author sets alike in any order and case; dates on the same calendar day), over items published in the twelve months before the run, from each source as far back as its archive reaches, with the rates also holding on the most recent six months, reported apart; and names the text extractor, with the rate at which it kept each structure class on that experiment's sample.

## If it stays unknown

Records keep X-001's region extractor for text; metadata is taken only from the tiers an approved design names, at first only what the page declares or the listing gives; a field no approved tier yields is left empty, with its provenance stated.

## Evidence so far

[[X-002]] measured every tier on 30 labelled items and suggested an order per field. [[X-003]] froze that order before any new label existed, and ran on 2026-09-29 over nine sources' April to September 2026 publications (CNA uncovered), testing it on 60 items no design choice had seen. It was not met: the order got title, every author and the Source publication time all right on 44 of 60 (Wilson 61 to 83 per cent), against 28 of 30 on the items it was chosen on. No other order tried did significantly better; X-002's order did significantly worse (30 of 60). Titles matched on 57 of 60 (56 under this question's match), publication times on 59 of 60 and authors on 47 of 60; 12 of the 16 misses were authors on two sources, where the owner labelled a credit from the page that no tier found. Six months held too few unseen items from the smallest sources: X-003's draw found 6 HiddenLayer items and 10 from METR in the window, all HiddenLayer's and 6 of METR's now labelled, so a further held-out test needs a longer window.

Held-out rates per tier, over the items where each gave a candidate:

| Field | declared | listing | visible | headline | heuristic | model | guessed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Title | 49 of 60 | 13 of 20 | 56 of 60 | 52 of 55 | — | 48 of 55 | 47 of 60 |
| Authors | 14 of 27 | — | 22 of 24 | — | — | 26 of 32 | 12 of 33 |
| Publication time | 20 of 20 | 33 of 33 | 32 of 36 | — | 39 of 47 | 42 of 43 | 41 of 60 |

For text, whose items were not held out (only the metadata labels were), the region extractor with markdownify kept, by script, at least 90 per cent of every structure class on 211 of 214 HTML items. Per class, the share of items keeping 90 per cent was 100 per cent for headings, tables, formulas, links, images, captions and block quotes, 99.4 for list items and 95.7 for code blocks. The owner confirmed the check on only 9 of 20: a region that was not the article body (3), content of the classes missing or broken that the script did not report (7), and a false loss (1). The scripted rate therefore overstates what a reader gets, and content a page builds only when a reader clicks is not in the page for any check to count.

What a next experiment would need to settle, each as a general mechanism rather than a rule for named sites: where a page credits its authors outside its header or renders the credit late; where an item's region ends, so that a page's own title is kept and a trailing list of links to other items is not; and a structure check that sees what the owner found missing.

[[X-004]] tested those mechanisms on 60 held-out labels over twelve months, 30 of them from the most recent six, on 2026-09-30. It was not met on metadata: the pre-registered order got title, every author and the Source publication time all right on 50 of 60 (54 needed; 49 on the labels as first submitted) and on 26 of the 30 recent (27 needed), against 56 of 60 on the development items. Titles matched on 56, dates on 58 and authors on 52. Of the ten misses:
- four are the model not applying credit rules the owner had settled (an organisation credited in an "Author" block, three; a subtitle's person taken over that block, one);
- two are items not kept, by a timeout and by a page template the selector missed;
- one is a quoted person;
- one is a joint advisory's 26 authoring organisations;
- two are titles.

None is a labelling error: a check of all 60 labels against the pages alone logged three corrections, and the decision review found a fourth (S002's date, changing no figure). Held-out rates per tier, over the items where each gave a candidate (whole window):

| Field | declared | listing | visible | headline | heuristic | model | guessed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Title | 46 of 58 | 15 of 22 | 54 of 58 | 54 of 55 | — | 35 of 55 | 45 of 58 |
| Authors | 17 of 24 | — | 22 of 25 | — | — | 28 of 32 | 13 of 27 |
| Publication time | 17 of 17 | 33 of 33 | 27 of 29 | — | 37 of 44 | 44 of 47 | 42 of 58 |

The headline, listing and model date tiers hold in both the recent and older six months. Structure met its bar on this sample, from configured sources: the owner confirmed all 18 verifiable items, exactly the threshold. By script, 99 per cent of HTML items kept at least 90 per cent of every class; per class, the share of items keeping 90 per cent was 100 per cent for headings, tables, formulas, images, captions and block quotes, 99.7 for list items and links, and 90.0 for code blocks. Raw pages were kept for 387 of 389. METR and HiddenLayer had no unseen items in the recent six months, and CNA remains uncovered.

The largest share of what remains, four of the ten misses, is the model's judgement in applying credit rules already settled, rather than rules still unsettled. Structure is not held out, and sources nobody has configured are untested.

[[X-005]] took up X-004's metadata misses on the same 60 labelled items, so it is a re-test on seen labels, not held out. It ran twice on 2026-10-01: its own design once, and then, after the owner changed the design and the labels it was scored on, a second design. A design and criterion changed after a result belong in a second experiment, so that second design and its test are recorded as [[X-006]], which supersedes X-005; X-005 keeps both runs as its record.

The first design stated the settled credit rules to the model and fixed the two title defects. It was not met: all three right on 54 of 60 (54 needed) and 28 of 30 recent (27 needed), but authors right on 54 of 60 (56 needed). Titles and dates were right on every page X-004 had kept. Three of its four authors misses on kept pages turned on the model's judgement of the credit: two credits it was shown and did not give, one false credit; the fourth was the joint list inside a body. Assessments on 90 development and check items found the model nearly deterministic but sensitive to any change in its input (about one answer in seven moved), copying names well and judging their role badly: it gave a false credit on 7 of 27 pages that credit no one, most often the site's own name. Two structural checks on its answer caught some of those and lost no real credit, but were shaped on development items. Showing it the page's markup, a second reading by agreement, and rewritten prompts each removed real organisation credits along with false ones.

The owner then redesigned the decision. The second design, now [[X-006]]'s:
- the site's own organisation is the publisher, never an author; authors are the people and other organisations a page credits;
- the author credit is read from the page's byline element by a selector per source, held as data beside the content selector, on the owner's assumption that configuring a source's page structure is a required upstream step, not yet designed;
- each field is decided by deterministic sources that must not contradict: for the title, the headline and declared metadata; for authors, declared metadata and the byline element; for the publication time, declared metadata and the listing, with the date heuristic as the fallback. Each value carries a status (agreed, single, fallback, disputed, empty, or model);
- no model decides a field a deterministic source has a value for. A targeted call on a disputed field is recorded and changes nothing; a last-resort call may fill an empty title or date, never authors. Neither call has been measured as a decider; both were off in this design's two test runs, and in development the escalation call ran only in advise mode, changing nothing.

The labels were reread once, mechanically, after the first run's misses had been seen, so that a labelled author that is the site's own name reads as the publisher (8 of the 60 test labels changed). This design's runs were scored on those reread labels, not on the hand labels as given. It ran twice on X-004's items, both on 2026-10-01 and with no model call: once inside X-005 as a recorded deviation, and once as [[X-006]], frozen separately, whose run reproduced the first in every compared figure (scores, per-item verdicts, statuses, selector rates and candidates). Both were met: all three right on 57 of 60 and authors on 57 of 60, both predicted before the run, and on all 30 recent, against a prediction of at least 27 for the first of the two runs and of 30 for the second. Titles and dates were right on 58. The three misses are the two items X-004 never kept and the joint list in a body. Of the 174 fields on kept pages, titles were agreed on 55 and single-source on 3; authors agreed on 16, single on 14, empty on 27 and disputed on 1; publication times agreed on 2, single on 49 and from the fallback on 7. The byline selector matched on every page of every source that has one except 3 of 7 openai pages, where it matched nothing.

The two runs are on different labels. Like for like, on the reread labels, the first design reads 51 of 60 and the second 57: five of the six items gained are the publisher rule applied to the output, and one is the model's false credit going. Two further items are right in the second run only because their labels were reread. So most of the gain is the rule, and what the mechanism adds is that no model decides and the output depends only on the page and the day of the run.

What this does and does not show. It shows that on these nine configured sources, title, authors and publication time can be decided without a model, on runs over items whose misses had been seen, with selectors and voter roles chosen on 90 items from the same sources. It does not show accuracy on unseen pages or on a source nobody has configured. Both runs were on one day, and the date heuristic's answer depends on the run day, so they do not show that a run on another day gives the same metadata. Against this question's "Answered when": neither X-005 nor X-006 reports a per-tier rate against the hand labels at all, on these 60 or on held-out items, only each field's status and the selector's match counts, so the byline-selector tier has no measured rate anywhere; the recent six months are reported for all three fields together, not per tier; and the text extractor's rate still rests on [[X-004]]. Open after it: joint author lists inside a body, where the labels of two sets disagree; the step that produces and checks a source's selectors; most publication times resting on a single source; and the date heuristic's dependence on the day of the run.
