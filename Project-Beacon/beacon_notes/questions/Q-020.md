# Q-020 How a source's link becomes a checked page-structure configuration

## What is unknown

Who or what produces the Entry parts of a source's Source Set entry ([[SRC-03]]) that say where on an item's page its body is found, and since [[X-006]] its credited authors, with the separators between names and the parts to drop? How is an entry checked before Collections use it, and how is drift noticed, when a site's redesign leaves such a part finding nothing or the wrong element without any fetch failing? [[Q-005]]'s answer put the body's part in each source's entry, and [[X-006]]'s design adds the byline's beside it, on the owner's assumption that configuring a source's page structure is a required step upstream, not yet designed. A part for credited authors that finds nothing gives an empty author, and a part for the body that finds the wrong element gives a wrong text; neither is a Failed item ([[SRC-12]], [[SRC-14]]) or a silent period ([[SRC-06]]). A Drift flag is now raised at a Cycle's close ([[SRC-16]], [[SRC-20]], [[SRC-21]]); how each Drift sign is judged is left to design.

The owner's envisioned candidate, of 2026-10-01, is that whoever adds a source provides only its link, and BEACON derives from it the structure knowledge the extraction design needs, so that the items wanted from that source can be extracted with it. That raises three further unknowns. Which links can be accepted, and on what test is one refused: what a source must offer (a page listing its items, pages readable without an account, a structure its items share) for the derivation to be possible at all? Can the structure be derived from the link alone, by what means, and how is what was derived verified before it is trusted? And where BEACON cannot derive or cannot verify some or all of an entry, what does it do: refuse the source, admit it with the fields it could establish, or hand it to a person, and what does the person who gave the link see?

## Why this priority

P1: every source BEACON adds, the ten of [[Q-005]] and each one a Curator admits after discovery ([[Q-003]]), needs this configuration before its metadata can be decided as [[X-006]] decides it. Collection can proceed meanwhile on entries whose parts are first written by hand and enter through an Onboarding and a Curator's acceptance, so it is not P0. It is not P2, because until how each Drift sign is judged is designed and measured, a broken part for credited authors can read like a page that credits no one, so wrong records can accumulate before any flag is raised, and because whether an entry holds such parts at all is a choice the Source Set's design rests on.

## Answered when

The owner approves a configuration step that names:
- what a person adding a source must provide, and whether a link alone suffices;
- which links are accepted and the test that refuses one, with the Refusal ground given to the person;
- what the person who gave the link sees in each outcome: accepted, refused, or configured only in part;
- who or what writes each Entry part, and the check an entry passes before Collections use it;
- what happens, per Entry part, when one cannot be derived or does not pass that check;
- how each Drift sign is judged, and so what raises a Drift flag.

And one experiment shows all of these:
- on at least two sources that appear in no earlier experiment, each configured by that step alone, the rate at which the decided authors and the kept body match the owner's hand labels, over at least 20 items per source, meets a bar the owner sets before the experiment runs and is reported per source, with the count of sources and of Entry parts the step could not configure and what it did with each;
- on links the acceptance test should refuse, at least one for each of the five observed Refusal grounds, it refuses each, naming its ground, and it refuses none of the sources above;
- on saved pages of the configured sources, altered so that each Entry part that locates something on a page in turn finds nothing or a different element, and so that a source whose part for credited authors is None offered prints a byline, the drift signal flags at least 95 per cent of the altered entries within the run that processed the altered pages, each kind of alteration reported apart, an entry that could not be altered left out and counted; and on the same pages unaltered it flags nothing, any flag reported with its cause.

## If it stays unknown

No configuration step is approved, so how the Entry parts that locate parts of a page are derived and checked, and how each Drift sign is judged, stay undesigned and unmeasured. What the rows require holds meanwhile: a source enters only by a Curator's acceptance of an Onboarding outcome ([[SRC-08]], [[SRC-28]]), and a change to an Entry part other than the source's name only by that or by a Curator's acceptance of a correction BEACON prepared from the source's pages ([[SRC-36]], [[SRC-39]]), parts first written by hand from the source's own pages, as [[X-006]]'s were, entering an Onboarding as a person's proposals; and a part that breaks raises a Drift flag at a Cycle's close once the rows' tests find it ([[SRC-16]], [[SRC-20]], [[SRC-21]]), each Drift sign judged by whatever means is built before this is answered. A source whose part for credited authors is None offered gets its authors from declared metadata only.

## Evidence so far

[[X-006]]'s byline selectors were found by hand on development pages from the nine configured sources. On X-004's 58 kept test pages each matched on every page of its source except 3 of 7 openai pages, where it matched nothing; no selector that decided an item's authors read a non-name. Two are fragile by construction: one rests on the element's position (hiddenlayer), one on styling classes (calif). arXiv's byline element holds affiliation marks and notes: [[X-005]] records its selector as right on only 3 of 6 check pages before a drop field was added after seeing check data, and after it as still disputing metadata on 2 and finding no byline element on 1. [[X-004]] lost one of its 60 test items (S204) to a page template its body selector missed. X-006 lists what a configured selector does not handle: a byline outside the element, a byline in a bare text node, a source with several templates, a byline injected only by script, and a source configured as having no byline that later starts printing one, which reports "no author" without notice. Its result claims nothing for a source nobody configured.
